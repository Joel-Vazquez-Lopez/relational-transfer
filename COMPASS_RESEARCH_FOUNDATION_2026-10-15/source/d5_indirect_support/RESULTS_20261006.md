# Role-transfer experiment: what survived

Follow-up: `AUDIT_COMPONENT_RESULTS_20261006.md` reports the crossed
evaluation of all 42 checkpoints and 16 selected component-control runs.

Terminology correction following the evaluation audit: uniform four-choice
chance is 25%, not 50%. References below to near-chance failures mean the
roughly 50% role-direction regime, not four-choice chance. The subsequent
crossed audit directly separates role and event errors. The 1280 entries
are balanced training configurations, not 1280 unique meanings.

Date: 2026-10-06. This note summarizes new work in
`PROSPECTIVE_20261006_PROTOCOL.md`, its validation addendum, and the
conditional independent confirmation plan. The earlier D5 near/far contrast
was strong but confounded; the earlier same-distance PATH2 study did not
support a universal shared-neighbor rule.

## Controlled comparison

We trained 30 small two-layer Transformer listeners: five previously unused,
nonisomorphic 10-entity, 4-regular graph layouts, three paired seeds, and two
withheld-pair conditions per seed. Both conditions had 1,280 matched training
configurations (80 distinct ordered-pair/event meanings) and the same
generated episode schedule after identity relabeling.
The withheld entities were never directly paired in training. Each final
model answered 1,024 new four-scene role questions. The paired conditions
had equal degree, shortest path two, and matched focal-endpoint triangle
counts; other graph properties were not all matched.

All 30 models exceeded 99.6% accuracy when we subsequently evaluated every
familiar training meaning. Held-pair accuracy ranged from 48.0% to 100%.
For example, in world `new2`, seed 10402, familiar accuracy was 100% for
both models, while held-pair accuracy was 99.5% versus 49.8%. This shows that
excellent familiar-example accuracy can coexist with a severe transfer
failure under matched data volume. Freshly generated test scenes reproduced
the world-level margin pattern.

The mean held correct-minus-reversed-role score differences (two common
neighbors minus one) across each world's three seeds were:

| World | Mean score difference |
| --- | ---: |
| new0 | -9.84 |
| new1 | +1.88 |
| new2 | -8.87 |
| new3 | +2.47 |
| new4 | -0.08 |

Thus the direction reverses across layouts and starts. One-versus-two common
neighbors is not a reliable law. The score is 10 times cosine similarity for
each candidate, so its correct-minus-reversed-role margin ranges from -20
to +20. A positive margin favors the correct role assignment.

## Internal predictor tests

The locked primary test asked whether focal query-vector separation at step
1,000 predicted the final paired score difference. It did not: Spearman
rho = 0.43 across 15 pairs, and the within-world permutation p = 0.193.
The planned familiar-performance gate passed. This early signal should not
be advertised as a validated predictor.

After observing the first nine outcomes, we noticed that step-3,000 query
separation agreed better with final transfer. In the full first set it
matched the sign in 13/15 pairs (descriptive rho = 0.87). We then used a
prewritten conditional rule to run six new pairs (12 models) on three further
graph layouts and new seeds. This independent test failed its required
5/6 sign agreement: only 3/6 signs matched. Its descriptive rho was 0.83,
and all three world-mean directions matched. The misses had small final
score differences, but a rule that ignores small differences was not
specified before this test. It requires a new independent test.

The confirmation models all exceeded 99.2% on the complete familiar set.
Their held accuracy ranged from 96.2% to 100%, and fresh scenes gave the
same world-level pattern. This confirmation set did not contain the severe
near-chance failures seen in the first set; it cannot validate detection of
such failures.

## Exploratory causal probe

After both predictor datasets were complete, we doubled the distance between
the two focal identity embeddings at evaluation time, keeping their midpoint
and every other weight fixed. We applied the same intervention to all 42
models and tested on new scenes. Five models had original held accuracy below
80%. All five gained a *small* amount of correct-minus-reversed-role margin,
with mean gain 0.12 on the -20 to +20 scale. Their mean held accuracy rose
about 1.2 percentage points, and none was rescued from near-chance
performance. Full familiar accuracy stayed above 99.9% in this low-transfer
subset. Across all 42 models, the mean margin change was -0.28. Thus simply
separating the focal embeddings is not an adequate repair or a demonstrated
mechanism for the large failures. The full per-model dose-response results
are in `results/embedding_intervention_20261006.json`.

## Scientific reading

We have a reproducible *phenomenon*: matching the amount of training and
nearby graph statistics does not ensure the same role transfer, and familiar
accuracy can hide severe failures. We do not yet have a confirmed general
predictor, a singled-out graph cause, or evidence that the effect occurs in
natural language or pretrained LLMs. The internal measures remain leads for
further experiments.

A sensible 15-credit question is: **When can a model trained on familiar
actor-patient relations fail on a new pair, and which features of the training
evidence or learned state distinguish the failures?** The controlled task
supports a rigorous answer even if a simple predictor fails. An authentic
sentence set and a pretrained-language-model pilot would test how far the
insight travels; their outcomes must be reported separately.

For publication, the next study would need an independent graph-family
replication of a *predefined, calibrated* risk score, a causal intervention
that changes transfer while retaining familiar performance, and a linguistic
evaluation with multiple forms and at least one pretrained model. Existing
work already studies representation-based predictors of compositional
generalization on other tasks, so our novelty would have to come from the
role-transfer/evaluation setting and a genuinely validated mechanism.
See [An and Du (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18858) and
[Fu and Frank (2024)](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.492/).
