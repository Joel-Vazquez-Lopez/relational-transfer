# B-D34 frozen discovery protocol: available versus used candidate information

Status: **discovery only**. Chosen after early B-D33 showed query-frozen learned-start switches can redirect preference while candidate-frozen CUE→P95 cannot. This tests whether candidate plasticity creates relational information or merely reorients already decodable information toward the task query.

Use the exact B-D21 600-scene 2×2 relation×cue grid. The six cyclic ordered identity pairs used in B-D27–D30 (120 scenes) are the held test; the other 24 pairs (480 scenes) fit. Extract final normalized candidate vectors from CUE step1000 and3000, P95 step1000 and3000, NO_CUE step3000, plus B-D33 query-frozen and candidate-frozen CUE→P95 step3000 for both seeds. No model training or probe-family selection.

Fit separate fixed-ridge linear readouts for the **relative event** and **cue sign** using all four cells of fitting scenes. Center features on fit only, fit intercept, ridge penalty 1.0 on feature weights (unpenalized intercept), and evaluate classification accuracy and continuous-label R2 on held identity pairs. The labels are balanced by construction. Report in-model score-grid relation/cue contrasts alongside probe outcomes. If CUE candidate states already decode relation well yet score ignores it, the mechanism is functional selection/alignment, not loss or creation of raw information. If the relation is absent even under this probe, candidate plasticity may create a linearly accessible relation; nonlinear access remains possible. Probes are descriptive and not causal proof. No protected/confirmation data or canonical mutation.
