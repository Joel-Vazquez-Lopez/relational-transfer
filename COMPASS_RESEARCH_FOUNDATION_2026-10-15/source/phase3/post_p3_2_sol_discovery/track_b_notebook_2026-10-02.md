## Starting problem

B-D11 showed that the original event renderer has a perfect source-only shortcut. B-D15–D18 showed that source-balanced scenes can force two-object information use, but high-performing seeds can implement it through different token paths. B-D19–D20 showed that geometry edits, even event-preserving ones, engage attention-weight changes. Thus neither a universal static-value relay nor a universal dynamic-routing event circuit survived. The next microscope needed to vary shortcut availability while holding relative geometry and model training conditions matched.

## B-D21: matched cue/no-cue world family

For every candidate scene from the source-balanced renderer, all three objects receive a common ±0.65 x translation. Relative trajectories are unchanged. In NO_CUE the sign is independent of event; in CUE it perfectly predicts event. The same latent scenes, identities, foils, candidate order, query messages, architecture, initial parameters, and batch-index schedule were used for matched seeds 3801 and 3802. The data, code, protocol, audit and hashes were frozen before training.

The source-position rule was 100% correct on CUE candidate events and 50.23% train / 49.56% independent test on NO_CUE. Both datasets balanced event labels and answer positions. Each condition had 12,000 training and 2,400 independent test episodes, with four candidates each. The CUE position shortcut still requires role/identity selection to solve the four-choice episode.

Both NO_CUE seeds reached 0.9975 independent four-choice accuracy at step3000; both CUE seeds reached 1.0000. The frozen 600-scene 2×2 relation × cue grid then produced a sharp divergence. NO_CUE followed relative event on 0.9817/0.9892 of cue–relation conflict cells (seeds 3801/3802). CUE followed the cue on 0.9983/1.0000 of conflict cells. Mean relation/cue event-score contrasts were ~22.93/−0.19 and ~20.77/0.15 in NO_CUE, versus ~0.45/22.15 and ~0.48/22.02 in CUE. Divergence was already strong at step1000. CUE conflict cells are off its *joint* training support, so this demonstrates its learned functional extension, not in-support failure under contradictions.

## B-D22: matched-world causal path audit

B-D22 froze three edits on the same scenes: relation-only (target relative event changes at fixed cue), cue-only (global translation changes at fixed relative event), and concordant diagonal (both change). Raw input edit norms were ~2.85, 4.50 and 5.21 respectively; these are not magnitude matched. Despite comparable event-query contrasts (~1.57–1.62), the candidate side strongly selected the useful variable. In NO_CUE, relation-only edits moved the candidate vector by ~1.42–1.52 and the event score by ~21–23; cue-only edits moved the vector by ~0.26–0.27 and the score by ~1.2 absolute. In CUE, relation-only vector changes were ~0.29–0.36 and score changes ~0.7–1.2 absolute; cue-only vector changes were ~1.57 and score changes ~22. A changed query bank is therefore not sufficient to explain the arm divergence; candidate encoding/readout alignment matters.

Post-block0 token patch pathways differed substantially by seed. NO_CUE seed3801 relation-only effect transferred more through target than source token (preference flips 0.582 versus 0.325); seed3802 transferred more through source and CLS (0.517 source, 0.417 CLS, 0.103 target). CUE cue-only effect was CLS-dominant in seed3802 (0.847 flip), but less so in seed3801 (0.192); neither is a unique universal implementation. Exact block1 pre-MLP decompositions show substantial attention-weight terms in some paths, yet fractions vary by seed and edit and are not additive causal shares.

The bounded result is **experience redirects functional sensitivity and candidate-state geometry under tightly matched relative scenes and training conditions**. That statement is still compatible with ordinary supervised shortcut selection, and it does not identify a reusable transformer-native operation.

## B-D23: noisy cue contradictions during acquisition

B-D23 reused the exact underlying B-D21 scenes and assigned the cue sign correctly with probability 0.95 or 0.99, using nested frozen random draws. P95 had 2,446 conflicting training candidates (5.10%); P99 had 475 (0.99%). Independent tests had 459 and 81 conflicts. The data, code, and outcomes were frozen before training. Two matched seeds were trained for 3,000 updates; all final independent task accuracies were 0.9917–0.9958.

On the fully supported cue–relation conflict grid, P95 relation accuracy rose from 0.246/0.262 at step1000 to 0.493/0.574 at step2000 and 0.693/0.672 at step3000. Its mean relation score contrast rose to 13.43/13.22 while cue contrast fell to 6.82/7.48. P99 remained mostly cue-driven: conflict relation accuracy 0.151/0.028 at step3000, with relation contrast 5.11/1.70 and cue contrast 14.78/17.06. Its two seeds were beginning to diverge despite identical symbolic condition and high ordinary accuracy.

This is evidence for a **developmental competition between a cheap cue and an exact relation**, controlled by the frequency of contradiction-bearing examples. It is not yet evidence that contradiction count alone is the causal variable: gradient mass, example position, optimizer history, and finite dataset reuse may matter. B-D24 was frozen to extend the exact P99 trajectories to 8,000 updates, with an exact step3000 state equality assertion, to distinguish slow acquisition from persistent shortcut reliance.

## B-D24: unchanged P99 training extended to 8,000 updates

The extension reproduced both B-D23 P99 step3000 states exactly, then continued the same optimizer and schedule. Seed3801 conflict relation preference increased from 0.151 at step3000 to 0.363 at step8000, with relation score contrast 5.11→9.54 and cue contrast 14.78→13.66. Seed3802 rose from 0.028 to only 0.047, with relation contrast 1.70→2.23. Independent task accuracy stayed ~0.99. More time under the same rare-contradiction schedule did not reliably make the model relational; optimization trajectory matters.

## B-D25: switch contradictory experience during acquisition

Both switch runs exactly reproduced their parent checkpoint at step1000 and retained optimizer state and batch-index schedule. Switching CUE→P95 introduced contradictions: conflict relation preference rose from ~0 at step1000 to 0.675/0.603 by step3000, near P95-from-start 0.693/0.672. Switching P95→CUE removed contradictions: 0.246/0.262 at step1000 fell to 0.023/0.018 by step3000. This intervention causally ties ongoing contradiction-bearing experience to relational functional sensitivity under this optimizer and synthetic task. Early learning was not irreversible in either direction. The manipulation changes both the support and frequency of training gradients, so it does not isolate a transformer-specific acquisition principle.

## B-D26: raw cross-model query/candidate recombination

B-D26 swapped event-query differences from each of six matched models into the candidate vector grid of each other model at step3000, with no adapter. NO_CUE versus CUE query-difference cosine was only 0.025/0.132 across matched seeds. Raw cross-arm recombinations often lost score margins and sometimes preference accuracy. This rejects an unqualified candidate-only sufficiency claim in the original coordinates. It leaves coordinate gauge and coadaptation as alternatives.

## B-D27: held-identity orthogonal query alignment

A single 48×48 orthogonal Procrustes map was fit using oracle query vectors from 24 ordered identity pairs, leaving six pairs out. It saw no candidate states, scores, held queries or geometry. Held event-query cosine improved from 0.058/0.147 raw to 0.797/0.738 aligned. On 120 held scenes, aligned donor queries restored recipient behavior: NO_CUE donor into CUE candidates followed cue on conflicts, while CUE donor into NO_CUE candidates followed relation at ~0.96–0.98. Score RMSE relative to the recipient was ~2.7–4.2, versus raw swaps ~9–12. This indicates substantial shared query semantics under a coordinate change and candidate-side choice of usable scene variable. The map has high capacity; it is not a discovered relational operation.

## B-D28: alignment information budget

Nested fits used 4, 8, 12, 18 or 24 identity pairs, with either both event queries or only their difference. At eight pairs, held event-query cosine was ~0.79 in seed3801 and ~0.60–0.62 in seed3802, and aligned swaps largely recovered each recipient's preference orientation. Full-query fits had effective ranks ~9–11 at eight pairs; held query row-span residuals were ~0.11–0.16. Event-difference fits were similar despite rank ~4–6 and somewhat higher residual in some directions. At 24 pairs full-query held residual reached numerical zero, consistent with a low-rank query bank. Eight examples therefore suffice for useful alignment, but Procrustes remains a high-capacity 48×48 transform and shared global event direction could explain the result. B-D29 froze pair-correspondence and event-correspondence nulls to challenge the pair-specific interpretation.

## B-D29: correspondence nulls

The correct eight-pair Procrustes match outperformed 32 frozen shuffled identity-pair matches and 32 wrong-event matches, in both directions, both seeds, and both full-query/event-difference fitting. Mean held event-query cosine was 0.79/0.60–0.62 under correct matching; pair shuffling reduced it to ~0.43/0.35; wrong-event mapping to ~0.00. Score RMSE to the recipient rose from ~2.4–5.4 to ~6.8–8.0 under pair shuffling and ~11.8–13.8 under wrong-event mapping. Thus the gauge is not merely any orthogonal map aligned to a global event axis. The finite query bank and model architecture may nevertheless supply a simple identity factorization.

## B-D30: source/target factorization of event-query differences

Using 24 ordered identity pairs to fit `intercept + source factor + target factor`, with six cyclic pairs held out, predicted held event-query differences with cosine 0.962–0.990 across NO_CUE, CUE, P95, P99 and both seeds. Held variance explained ranged 0.786–0.963; seven of eight fits exceeded 0.92, while NO_CUE seed3801 was 0.786. A source-only or target-only fit was less consistent and sometimes poor. Thirty-two pair-label permutation fits had held cosine means ~0.43–0.54 and maxima ≤0.76. Candidate score RMSE under the additive prediction was ~0.69–3.17; held preference orientation largely survived. This is a strong compact *descriptive* code for query differences across identities, but no acquired operation has been shown.

## B-D31: additive query code through training

At the exact seeded random initialization, the same held-pair additive fit already had cosine 0.994/0.993 and held variance explained 0.732/0.802. The random query differences were mostly aligned with each other (mean pairwise cosine 0.944/0.927). By step3000, additive held cosine stayed high (0.962–0.990), while pairwise cosine fell substantially (~0.34–0.43 for most arms), and trained event-query directions had low cosine to their initialized directions (~0.14–0.40). Training expanded/rotated query geometry but did not create the source+target factorization from scratch. This sharply weakens any claim that the additive query code itself is an acquired transformer-native relational grammar. The oracle message is a four-token concatenation of source, target, event and padding symbols, so additive factorization is also an expected architectural/input-code prior, though its quantitative persistence was measured rather than assumed. B-D32 freezes one listener branch at initialization during learning to test which branch changes are needed for cue/relation selection.

## B-D32: cold-start branch surgery

Eight new NO_CUE/CUE models froze either the query branch or candidate branch at the exact random initialization and trained the other branch with the matched B-D21 schedule. Frozen parameter hashes were checked throughout. All failed the predeclared 0.95 ordinary task-accuracy gate: final four-choice accuracies were 0.488–0.550 with query frozen and 0.388–0.390 NO_CUE / 0.668–0.677 CUE with candidate frozen. Query-fixed NO_CUE nevertheless developed a strong paired-grid relation preference (0.921/0.969), and both branch-fixed CUE arms mostly followed cue on that grid. These score-grid tendencies do not establish task learning because role/foil selection was poor. The failure is compatible with weak untrained features, optimization difficulty, or a need for coadaptation. It does not prove a unique two-branch operation. B-D33 was frozen after early B-D32 failures to repeat branch surgery only after a successful 1,000-update learned start.

## B-D33: learned-start branch surgery during cue-reliability switches

All eight branches exactly reproduced their parent seed/arm step1000 model and retained the optimizer trajectory until the switch; the frozen branch SHA remained equal afterward. In CUE→P95, freezing the query branch while allowing candidate learning produced final P95 test accuracy 0.9896/0.9912 and conflict relation preference 0.678/0.598. Freezing candidate while allowing query learning gave P95 accuracy 0.9529/0.9504 and conflict relation preference only 0.004/0.006, near a cue-only strategy. The candidate-frozen models met the predeclared 0.95 task gate by a thin margin because most P95 scenes still have a correct cue. Their relation score contrast stayed ~0.3, versus ~11–13 when candidate could adapt. The unfrozen B-D25 CUE→P95 relation preference was 0.675/0.603, close to the query-frozen outcome.

For P95→CUE, query-frozen continuation reached cue-test accuracy 0.999/1.000 and reduced conflict relation preference to 0.020/0.018. Candidate-frozen continuation also reached cue-test ~0.998 but retained more relation preference, 0.136/0.133. Thus candidate plasticity is sufficient for rapid redirection in either switch, while query plasticity alone did not acquire the relation-sensitive extension from the CUE start. This is branch-specific causal evidence in a learned state, not proof of a unique parameter or circuit. Candidate-fixed failure may reflect the CUE candidate state's poor usable relation geometry rather than an inability of the query architecture in principle.

## B-D34: held-identity linear candidate readout

A fixed ridge readout fit on 24 ordered identity pairs and tested on six held pairs decoded relative event from CUE final candidate vectors at only 0.55–0.60 (held R² ~0.02–0.05) in both seeds/steps, while cue sign decoded at ~0.99. P95 final vectors yielded relative-event accuracy 0.863/0.892; NO_CUE 0.979/0.965. The query-frozen CUE→P95 candidate vectors reached 0.883/0.833, while candidate-frozen vectors stayed exactly at their CUE step1000 readout, 0.550/0.565. These are stronger evidence that contradiction-bearing experience changes the candidate representation rather than just rotating a query readout. A linear probe cannot establish information absence.

## B-D35: fixed nonlinear candidate readout

An RBF SVC with frozen gamma rule and C=1 or10 tested the alternative that CUE contains easily accessible but nonlinear relation information. CUE final held relative-event accuracy was only ~0.52–0.62; query-frozen CUE→P95 was ~0.82–0.88, P95 ~0.85–0.89, and NO_CUE ~0.91–0.98. CUE fit accuracy at C=10 was only ~0.68–0.69, so failure was not solely held-pair transport. This rules out the particular low/medium-capacity RBF decoder as a rescue. Other nonlinear decoders or earlier token states could retain information. B-D36 tests whether a non-attention candidate architecture reproduces the same broad learning pattern.

## B-D36: non-attention candidate architecture control

The candidate Transformer (38,976 parameters) was replaced by a permutation-invariant DeepSets encoder (24,816 parameters); the oracle-message Transformer query branch and cosine scorer remained. Frozen data, seeds, batch schedule and optimizer matched B-D21/B-D23. No architecture or hyperparameter adjustment followed outcomes. Both CUE models reached 0.998 independent task accuracy and followed cue on 100% of conflict-grid preferences. Both P95 models reached 0.980/0.968 task accuracy and showed partial relation preference on conflicts, 0.473/0.296. Their held-identity linear relative-event readout was 0.854/0.773, versus 0.517/0.540 in CUE. Thus **candidate self-attention is not necessary for the qualitative cue-reliability effect or the rise of a linearly readable relation in this microscope**. The P95 effect was weaker than in the original candidate Transformer at the same step.

NO_CUE DeepSets task accuracies were only 0.907/0.923 at step3000, below the predeclared 0.95 mechanism gate, although paired-grid relation preference was ~0.94/0.93. Its probe values (~0.90) are descriptive only and should not be used as evidence of complete task learning. The DeepSets control is smaller and has different inductive bias; it rejects necessity of candidate attention for the CUE/P95 contrast but does not prove all quantitative effects are architecture-independent. The query branch is still a Transformer, so this is not a whole-system architecture comparison.

## Decision and strongest surviving picture after B-D36

The original candidate-state operation hypothesis remains dead. Today's strongest *acquisition* finding is narrower: in matched source-balanced scenes, reliable cheap position cue training produces a candidate code that is excellent for the cue but weakly informative about relative event at its final vector. Contradiction-bearing experience drives a relation-sensitive candidate code and relation-aligned scores. The learned-start switch shows that candidate adaptation alone can drive this change with the query branch frozen, while query adaptation alone from a CUE start cannot, even though ordinary accuracy stays around 0.95. This is a causal branch-level statement under the particular architecture, optimizer, task and 1,000→3,000-update schedule. It does not specify a unique token circuit, state operation, or universal grammar.

The event-query bank has a compact source+target additive description that transports across held identity pairs, and matched identity correspondences matter. Yet the factorization is already strong at random initialization and likely reflects the symbolic input code plus architecture; training rotates/expands it. Cross-model alignment needs a fitted orthogonal gauge and is not a low-complexity action law. The candidate branch determines which scene variable is functionally useful in the aligned score, but that is a property of this two-encoder scorer, not an acquired transformation law.

The non-attention candidate control reproduces the key cue/P95 representational shift. This directly weakens a specifically Transformer-candidate explanation; ordinary supervised feature allocation remains the stronger null. B-D24 also showed rare contradictions do not reliably win merely with more time, and B-D25 showed recent training can reverse the direction. Optimization history and inductive bias therefore set quantitative outcomes, with no single scalar contradiction count or deterministic representation rule established.

**Stopping condition 4: further work in this world family has diminishing information value for Track B's actual objective.** More checkpoint probes, capacities and cue probabilities would refine the same generic shortcut-learning effect. A new Track B microscope should require transformations/compositions that cannot be solved by a one-feature cue or simple event classifier, include a non-attention whole-system baseline from the outset, and freeze a prospective *functional* law with held combinations and interventions. Today's bounded acquisition result may support Track A or a future control, but it is not a Transformer-native operation ready for independent confirmation. All B-D21–D36 evidence remains discovery-only; P3.2-E and protected confirmation material remain untouched.
