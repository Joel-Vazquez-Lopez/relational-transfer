# Track-B Discovery Brief — B00

**Status:** Discovery-only interpretation and action audit, 1 October 2026. **Canonical endpoint:** P3.2-E, unchanged. This brief proposes a next test; it does not execute one. The new read-only audit is `b00_action_audit.py` / `b00_action_audit.json` in this directory. It used only the twelve permitted A/B/C/D × 1701–1703 P3.0-R/P3.2 shards. No checkpoint was run, no canonical file was changed, and no protected or confirmatory material was accessed.

## 1. What the existing action atlas actually contains

The task has a speaker/message/query path and an independent three-object candidate encoder. Candidate scores are **10 times cosine similarity** between their final vectors. An oracle message is used in the P3.0-R/P3.2 atlas, so a given `(source identity, target identity, event)` supplies the same query input and final query vector regardless of candidate scene. The learned candidate encoder receives object identity one-hots and four xy observations per object; it has learned slot positions and a CLS readout. Thus object-slot invariance is learned/approximate, not enforced by the architecture.

There are 12 permitted discovery checkpoints and 128 bases per checkpoint. P3.0-R acquired BASE plus nine old actions for each base: 15,360 rows. Its indexed shards include raw query/candidate inputs, scores, token states after both transformer blocks, and final query/candidate vectors. P3.2 acquired six new actions per base: 9,216 rows, including exact inputs and scores, but **no hidden-state archive for those new actions**.

| Action | Exact input edit, always anchored to BASE | Existing joint measurements |
|---|---|---|
| I | Replace the oracle query source with a third distinct identity. Candidate scenes stay fixed. | I, IE, IC, IEC |
| E | Flip the oracle query event. Candidate scenes stay fixed. | E, IE, EC, IEC; later EY, REY |
| C | Cyclically permute the three object slots in every candidate, preserving each complete object row, answer position and candidate order. | C, IC, EC, IEC |
| R | Reverse oracle query source and target. Candidate scenes stay fixed. | R; later RY, REY |
| X | Replace each candidate's distractor with the *lowest* spare identity while retaining the two relational endpoints and geometry. | X only |
| Y | Replace each candidate's distractor with the *highest* spare identity. | Y, RY, EY, REY, VY |
| V | Rotate complete candidate xy trajectories by 90 degrees; identities and relational meaning are retained. | V, VY |

The P3.0-R joint grid is **IE, IC, EC, IEC**. The P3.2 joint grid is **RY, EY, REY, VY**. The new predictor used X as a proxy for Y and C as a proxy for V; those are modeling assumptions, not physical equivalences. In the B00 audit, within-checkpoint score-effect correlations averaged only **0.408 for X/Y** and **0.068 for C/V**.

**Crucial compositional distinction:** all archived joint conditions are independently constructed *unions of edits applied to the original input*. They are not repeated applications of a learned state operator, and the atlas never observes `M_b(M_a(C))`. I and X/Y refer to base-specific identity metadata, so an ordered input composition must be defined afresh before claiming an algebra. Input edits with genuinely clear compositions include slot cycles (`C²`, `C³`) and xy rotations (`V²`, `V³`, `V⁴`); V and Y commute as raw-input edits if Y is defined by current endpoint identities. Potentially informative unmeasured combinations include `CV`, `RV`, `EV`, `IV`, `CX`, `XY`, `V²`, and `V²Y`; ordered role replacements need additional disambiguation. **Unmeasured** means absent from the existing action atlas, not necessarily a fresh confirmatory universe: any new outcome generated here is discovery-only.

Previous predictors had differing access. P3.1 fitted old intervention score deltas from rich **pre-intervention** states, with action flags and source splits; its T6 withheld IEC only from its fitting, not from the archive. P3.2 Stage A used old responses and/or prestate, and then predicted the six newly acquired P3.2 actions before their outcomes. H_RESPONSE spent **nine measured old score consequences per target base** to construct eight response classes and an additive proxy-action table. H_STATIC used activation state; H_SEMANTIC had privileged task labels; H_DISTRIBUTED used a rich state kernel. Their errors are not equal-information comparisons.

## 2. Adversarial constraints from existing results

- **P2.9-E:** its R3 pairwise schemes were beneficial for IE, IC and EC in all 12 models (mean NRMSE approximately 0.51, 0.56 and 0.49 in the primary table), yet the stronger IEC triple boundary failed badly (triple NRMSE about 14.8). Matched-distance and control tests did not license a clean factor law. Pairwise interpolation is therefore an inadequate criterion for an operation.
- **P3.1-C:** familiar held-out score responses had predictive interaction structure: G2 T0 NRMSE 0.798 versus additive G0 0.981. But the semantic comparator achieved 0.730 and a generic RBF 0.774; control specificity failed. On withheld IEC, G1/G2 were 1.164 versus additive 0.930. A low-dimensional fitted interaction, or the proposed 1/42 gate, is not a privileged internal operation.
- **P3.2-E:** response-near/activation-far matched bases had lower new-action response distance than activation-near/response-far bases (1.531 versus 3.421, all 12 models). H_RESPONSE predicted the six new actions at 0.891 NRMSE overall, close to H_SEMANTIC 0.856; but H_RESPONSE failed badly on REY (1.141 versus semantic 0.596), gained little on the new single-action domain (1.011), and all **8/8** old response classes split under the expanded family. Response geometry is useful for finding hypotheses. A fixed eight-class ontology or global action law is not established.
- **B00 read-only audit:** fixed BASE-correct score effects have RMS 0.249 for C, 1.044 for V, 1.362 for X and 1.387 for Y, versus 5.450 for I, 8.608 for E and 6.980 for R. The score-level IE additive residual has RMS 6.692, whereas IC, EC and the IEC three-way inclusion-exclusion residual have RMS 0.235, 0.344 and 0.298. These numbers are descriptive, not latent interactions: the final cosine readout can itself make a change in query direction nonadditive. Most importantly, repeated BASE query vectors with the same oracle meaning are **bitwise identical** (max observed Euclidean difference 0). Query-only transport across candidate contexts would be a lookup result in this atlas.

The B00 pattern does **not** show one uniform “nuisance operation”: C nearly cancels, V has a larger effect, and X/Y effects correlate only moderately. It also does not show a meaningful triple latent gate merely because a triple input was present.

## 3. Candidate notions and their strongest nulls

| Candidate notion | Possible operational law | Strongest mundane explanation | Existing discrimination / status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Candidate-state transport under input transformations** | At a specified candidate-encoder stage, an action-indexed, low-complexity map `T_a` predicts the state under `a` and `T_b(T_a(h))` predicts an unmeasured ordered composition; downstream scores follow after resuming the frozen model. The map may depend on a small, specified functional context rather than a global linear matrix. | Smooth geometry of any encoder, raw xy rotation, training augmentation, or generic nonlinear regression; a map may encode action IDs or memorize scenes without learning relational computation. | C's weak score effect is compatible with approximate slot invariance; V/Y have nontrivial score effects. No existing `V`/`Y` hidden states or ordered state composition are measured. **Highest information-gain next target, not currently established.** |
| **B. Functional response-state transition** | A continuous old-action response fingerprint, rather than eight classes, indexes a transition law that predicts responses to a withheld action and its composition; contexts with matched fingerprints share the law. | Similarity/nearest-neighbor regression and task-semantic labels explain the same predictions; the fingerprint costs nine prior interventions and can split indefinitely as actions are added. | P3.2 matched-pair advantage supports functional equivalence locally, but REY failure and 8/8 class splits kill the simple fixed-class law. Viable only as a *conditional* law with explicit complexity and new-action tests. |
| **C. Query-side role/event operation** | Transform the query vector for role reversal or event flip and compose such maps across meanings/seeds. | A 60-meaning deterministic oracle-message codebook; low-cardinality semantic lookup and the cosine score readout. | The B00 exact same-meaning query equality makes context transport uninformative. P2.7 and P2.9 undermine clean role/event factorization. Low priority unless tested on genuinely unseen meanings or non-oracle messages. |
| **D. Query–candidate matching/binding rule** | A small relation-indexed subcomputation reads candidate trajectories into a query-compatible state and predicts score changes under role/event edits plus candidate transformations. | The known final cosine similarity already defines a matching rule; a flexible predictor can fit query/candidate vector pairs without identifying any reusable internal binding. | Large IE score residual and opposite P3.2 errors for VY versus REY suggest context dependence, but the exact cosine readout can generate much of this. Requires causal mid-layer evidence and a withheld composition before being named an operation. |

The ranking separates **empirical support** from **next-test value**. B has the strongest existing behavioral clue, but its simple version has already failed. A currently has the best chance of a decisive falsification because the raw transformations have explicit algebra and the candidate encoder has internal states that can be resumed. No candidate yet deserves the unqualified name `M`.

## 4. Smallest useful next composition test — proposed, not run

I would start with **candidate-state transport**, and try to kill it with one paired action family. Define `V` as a +90° rotation of every xy trajectory and `Y` as the highest-spare distractor replacement on the current candidate. Choose a specific candidate-encoder stage (post-block-1 CLS is the first choice, with the token tensor kept available for a null). On a predeclared subset of permitted discovery models and freshly generated discovery scenes, acquire only BASE, V and Y states. Fit a low-capacity `T_V` and `T_Y` from those single-action states, with a fixed complexity budget and no access to `V²`, `VY` or `V²Y` outcomes. Freeze the fitting choices and predicted states/scores **before** acquiring `V²Y` on disjoint scenes. The primary algebraic prediction is `T_Y(T_V(T_V(h))) ≈ T_V(T_V(T_Y(h))) ≈ h(V²Y)`; the two orders must agree where the input edits commute. Use `V²` and `VY` as diagnostic intermediate checks, without tuning after their outcomes. Do not count the already archived P3.2 VY scores as prospective evidence.

The **kill criterion** should be based on held-out score and resumed-network consequences, not only vector distance. Compare the predicted post-block-1 state patched into the untouched frozen candidate encoder with (i) the actual `V²Y` state/consequence, (ii) identity and additive-delta maps, (iii) matched-size generic nonlinear state regression, (iv) a task-semantic/geometry predictor, and (v) random/magnitude-matched and wrong-action patches. Split by scene, held-out identity relation and seed; otherwise a high-capacity state map can memorize geometry or meaning. Predefine success thresholds and uncertainty after inspecting only training/validation singles, then acquire the withheld outcomes once. If the map predicts final scores but patching its state does not have the specified downstream effect, it is a surrogate, not an identified computation.

This is a **B-D discovery design**, not yet a frozen B-P protocol. The archived VY scores have already influenced candidate choice, so a B-P confirmation would require a separate untouched action/context specification and its exact preregistration. The immediate next step, after review of this brief, is to build the B-D single-action candidate-side state atlas and test whether any such law survives its nulls. It is not to train new models or claim cross-domain grammar.

## 5. What would make me abandon a compact operation here

I would abandon the *compact-operation hypothesis for this model/task/action family* if, after a small predefined portfolio of genuinely different action algebras (e.g. candidate rotation/distractor transport and a separately defined relation-changing edit), each proposed law either (a) loses to identity, semantic/raw-geometry or capacity-matched generic predictors on unseen compositions; (b) fails cross-scene/seed transfer; (c) has no causal effect under stage-specific patches beyond matched controls; or (d) needs action-specific/high-capacity exceptions that grow roughly with every new composition. A single failed `V²Y` law only kills candidate A as specified. It cannot prove that no compact operation exists in any transformer. The scientifically honest outcome may be a distributed, context-dependent computation whose useful predictive summaries are not closed under composition.

## 6. Literature collision and novelty boundary

The abstract idea of a distributed causal variable, learned state transformation, role binding or compositional internal representation is **not novel by itself**. [Geiger et al., 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v236/geiger24a.html) give distributed alignment search and interchange-intervention validation; their [causal abstraction framework](https://jmlr.org/beta/papers/v26/23-0058.html) explicitly includes mechanism transformations. [Wu, Geiger and Millière, 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wu25j.html) identify a transformer variable-binding mechanism through causal interventions and developmental analysis. [Soulos et al., 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.blackboxnlp-1.23/) infer compositional vector structure and intervene on hidden embeddings. [Cohen and Welling, 2016](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/cohenc16.html) make the equivariance/homomorphism standard precise; our architecture does not guarantee it. [Kumon and Yanaka, 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.432/) show that even a causally relevant subnetwork and syntactic features can coexist with a non-compositional algorithm. A recent [homomorphism-error study](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v282/an26a.html) also narrows novelty for merely correlating representation composition with generalization. Therefore a valuable claim here would need an **identified, causally effective, prospectively predictive law with a defined information budget and failure boundary**, not just another probe, cluster, vector arithmetic, or claim of “transformer grammar.”

## 7. Provenance and decision

The exact input paths and SHA-256 hashes for B00 are in `b00_manifest.json`; `b00_plan.json` records that the audit was selected before its results. B00 measures descriptive action effects and query-vector identity after P3.2-E; it is **discovery-only**. The canonical P2.9/P3.1/P3.2 decisions remain unchanged. Current decision: **no established reusable operation; candidate-side transport is the most falsifiable next target, while response geometry is a useful source of context hypotheses. Stop here before any prospective test.**
