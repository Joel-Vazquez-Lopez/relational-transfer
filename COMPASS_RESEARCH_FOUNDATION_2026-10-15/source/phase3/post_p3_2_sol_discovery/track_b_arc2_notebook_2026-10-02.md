## B-D37: first affine world rejected before training

The first design used continuous 2D point states and ±90° rotations `R_s` and continuous translations `T_v`, with R→T held. Its frozen audit showed no type leakage, exact overlap or obvious unary solution; the strongest listed restricted baseline was commutative average at R²≈0.805. But after the audit, an algebraic shortcut became clear: held R→T is exactly `R_s x+v`, a sum of independently learnable single-action contributions. That would not force the model to apply one transformation to the state produced by another. The design was rejected **before any B-D37 model training**, logged in `bd37_post_audit_rejection.md`, and left intact. This was an audit failure despite the predeclared checks passing, not a positive result.

## B-D38: noncommuting held composition audit

The redesigned training world includes single R/T, R→R, T→T and R→T, each with 20,000 continuous examples; all T→R pairs are held. The held oracle is `R_s(x+v)=R_s x+R_s v`, which differs from additive wrong-order `R_s x+v` by `(R_s-I)v`. Independent tests contain 4,000 per family. The frozen audit found no T→R training example, no exact train/test duplicate, oracle error ~6×10⁻⁸ after float32 storage and exact regeneration. Rotation signs and translation means were near balanced. Held restricted baseline R² values: x-only ~0, action-only 0.387, last-R-only 0.605, additive wrong order 0.209, commutative average 0.802. No listed non-oracle baseline reached the 0.9 gate. This world requires a sign-dependent translation interaction at the *function* level; it does not identify an internal operator.

## B-D39: first whole-system architecture comparison

Two 64-dimensional, two-block Transformer regressors and two approximately sized four-hidden-layer MLPs were trained for 3,000 updates on the exact B-D38 training data. Architecture, seeds3901/3902, optimizer, batch schedule, endpoints and held criteria were frozen before training and withheld readout. All four achieved near-perfect in-support two-action R² (~0.999). **All failed held T→R**. Transformer held R² was 0.215/0.206; MLP held R² −1.694/−1.512. Transformer predicted commutator magnitude was only ~0.05 of truth, with cosine 0.155/−0.049. MLP commutator magnitude was ~1.6× truth but poorly directed. Good observed-family fit therefore supplied no systematic off-support composition in either architecture, and their extrapolations differed sharply.

## B-D40: exact signature of failed extension

B-D40 was selected after B-D39 and frozen before reading this diagnostic. Transformer predictions on held T→R were extremely close to the **wrong-order additive** expression `R_s x+v`: MSE ~0.001 to that expression, versus ~0.427/0.431 to the true oracle. This explains their held R²≈0.21 almost exactly. MLP predictions were not close to that extension (MSE ~1.16–1.19), nor to a comparably simple predeclared extension; first-T-only was closest among the listed baselines (~0.56–0.71). The Transformer has a strong, coherent but wrong compositional prior in this setting. The MLP has a different extrapolation, not evidence of superior generalization. This is a functional description of outputs; it does not identify an attention circuit or prove lack of latent operations.

## B-D41–D42: one-axis bridge, orthogonal-axis test

Chosen after B-D39/B-D40 to ask whether limited composite experience induces a transferable interaction. B-D41 replaced 5,000 R→T training examples with T→R examples having translation only on the x-axis. The primary held test uses T→R translations only on the y-axis; independent x-axis bridge and original full-plane T→R tests are retained. Support and restricted-baseline audit passed: no y-axis T→R training row or exact train/test duplicate, float32 oracle error ~6×10⁻⁸, balanced signs, and strongest restricted commutative-average R²≈0.877 on held y-axis. B-D42 froze matched Transformer/MLP training, checkpoint readout, and y-axis transfer gate before training.

Both architectures learned the x-axis bridge (R²≈0.999 at step3000) and retained excellent original-family fit. Neither passed orthogonal-axis transfer. Transformers reached y-axis R² 0.581/0.540, full-plane R² 0.668/0.633, and y commutator magnitude ~0.23/0.20 of truth. MLPs reached y-axis R² 0.875/0.864, full-plane R² 0.898/0.889, and y commutator cosine 0.869/0.840 with magnitude near one. Yet MLP y-axis MSE 0.055/0.060 was about as large as the commutative-average baseline 0.054, far above the frozen <0.027 gate. High commutator cosine by itself therefore overstated functional transfer. The two flat architectures respond differently to the same narrow bridge, but a single trained composite direction was insufficient for an accurate reusable action law.

## B-D43: shared-transition positive control

A ~51k-parameter MLP transition was applied to action1 and then action2 with **the same weights**; PAD deterministically skips the second step. Rotation and translation formulas were not hard-coded. It trained on the original B-D38 no-bridge examples at the same seeds/schedule and met the predeclared composition gate in both seeds. Full-plane held T→R R² was 0.9955/0.9960 at step500 and 0.9996/0.9998 at step3000; held y-axis R² at step3000 was also ~0.9996/0.9998. This proves the data can support a learned reusable step rule under a sequential parameter-sharing architecture. It does **not** prove the flat Transformer or MLP learned one; the architectural template supplies reuse and order by construction. The contrast isolates an inductive-bias boundary, not Transformer superiority or a universal neural law.

## B-D44–D45: matched bridge exposure versus composition-parameter span

B-D44 was chosen after the one-axis transfer failure and shared-step positive control. It created two 100,000-row worlds by replacing the same 10,000 R→T rows with the same 10,000 latent T→R bridge scenes. RANK1 has all bridge translations `(u,0)`; RANK2 uses 5,000 `(u,0)` and 5,000 `(u/√2,u/√2)`. Models see the same x, signs, translation norms, non-bridge rows, row order and batch schedule; only the direction of half the bridge translations changes. The audit verified bridge matrix ranks 1/2, exact norm matching (<6×10⁻⁸ difference), balanced signs, no held y-axis training row or exact duplicates. B-D45 froze Transformer/MLP training and held y-axis/full-plane tests before fitting.

RANK2 transferred strongly across both architectures and seeds. At step3000, held y-axis R² was 0.988/0.984 for Transformers and 0.992/0.996 for MLPs; full-plane T→R R² was 0.984/0.979 and 0.988/0.996. All four passed the frozen strong span-transfer gate. The exposure-matched RANK1 arms learned x-axis bridge at R²≈0.997–1.000 but held y-axis R² stayed 0.531/0.557 for Transformers and 0.815/0.773 for MLPs. Rank2 models also fit the independent diagonal bridge test at R²≈0.999. Thus *where* composite examples vary matters much more than their count in this setting; a two-direction bridge permits a highly accurate out-of-direction extension. This is a prospective adaptive-discovery contrast, not independent confirmation or evidence of explicit state operators.

## B-D46: bilinear regression destroys the operation inference

B-D46 was frozen after seeing the B-D45 neural success. A tiny explicit predictor learned a separate affine state map `A_s x+b_s` from single-R training rows, then a composite translation coefficient `B_s v` from only T→R bridge rows. It saw no held y/full-plane target, network state, or known rotation matrix. On RANK2, this minimum-norm regression attained essentially exact R²=1.000 on both held y-axis and full-plane T→R. On RANK1, it fit the x-axis bridge exactly but held y-axis R² was 0.754 and full-plane R² 0.807 because the second translation direction was underdetermined. This reproduces the qualitative rank contrast without applying a learned transition sequentially. The neural RANK2 success is therefore fully compatible with ordinary low-degree response-surface identification.

## Arc II decision after B-D46: current affine microscope is non-identifying

**What survived:** the transformation task has a real held ordered composition and no easy unary/order-insensitive solution under the frozen audits. Flat Transformer and MLP systems can perfectly fit observed action families while extrapolating very differently to an absent order. In this architecture, the Transformer defaults almost exactly to wrong-order additive composition. A shared-step architecture, with reuse/order supplied by design but action maps learned, generalizes nearly perfectly without composite examples. Limited composite experience on only one translation direction does not transfer accurately to an orthogonal direction. Matched bridge exposure across two independent directions yields strong held transfer in both flat architectures.

**What died:** B-D37's first held R→T task (exact additive shortcut); any claim that saturated observed-family performance implies composition; any claim that a high commutator cosine establishes functional transfer; a simple one-axis bridge sufficiency principle; and the inference that B-D45's rank2 neural success reveals an internal reusable operator. The explicit B-D46 bilinear null predicts the same rank effect exactly.

**Why this microscope stops:** for the affine rotation/translation family, `R_s(x+v)=A_s x+B_s v` is simultaneously the external composition law and a low-parameter direct response surface. Behavioral tests on any number of x/v samples cannot distinguish those internal organizations once the model realizes the same function. More seeds, translation directions or probes here would refine implementation details without solving this extensional non-identifiability. The Arc II mandate's **experimental non-identifiability** stop condition is met for this world. A future microscope should use a nonlinear, genuinely state-dependent second action for which a low-degree independent contribution model makes distinct predictions on held compositions, while retaining whole-system architecture controls and a sequential positive control. That is a redesign requirement, not a claimed discovery of a Transformer-native grammar. Everything in B-D37–D46 remains discovery-only; P3.2-E and protected confirmation material remain untouched.
