# Arc II B-D44 frozen rank-one versus rank-two composition support

Status: **discovery only**. Chosen after B-D42 showed a one-axis bridge was learned but did not transport fully and B-D43 showed a shared-step model succeeds on no-bridge data. The prediction at risk is that flat models need composition-conditioned parameter variation spanning the transformation's input space; raw bridge count alone should not suffice.

Construct two matched 100,000-row training conditions from B-D38. Replace the **same** deterministic 10,000 R→T examples with the **same** 10,000 latent T→R scenes (same x, rotation signs, translation magnitudes `u`, row order) in both arms. In **RANK1**, all bridge translations are `(u,0)`. In **RANK2**, the first 5,000 are `(u,0)` and the remaining 5,000 are `(u/√2,u/√2)`. Thus total bridge exposure, `|v|` distribution, non-bridge examples, labels for unchanged rows, model initialization and schedule are matched; only the span/orientation of composite translation experience differs. Use RNG 20264401 for bridge latents and 20264403 for replacement/shuffle.

The primary test is the fresh B-D41 y-axis T→R set `(0,u)`, absent from both training arms; the original B-D38 full-plane T→R is secondary. Audit exact train/test duplication, support directions, rank of bridge translation matrix, norm matching, oracle regeneration, sign balance, and restricted y-axis baselines. No model may train if an arm contains a nonzero y-only bridge row or if exact duplicate leakage occurs.

**Prospective prediction:** If the learned composite correction is linear in v and training support span is the limiting factor, RANK2 should outperform RANK1 on held y-axis and full-plane T→R, with similar in-support and bridge-fit accuracy. This is not an assertion that the model explicitly represents an operator. If RANK2 fails despite fitting both lines, the simple span principle is falsified for these architectures/schedule. If it succeeds, a new direction and architecture remain needed to distinguish bilinear interpolation from a reusable computational operation. All discovery-only; no protected/confirmation data.
