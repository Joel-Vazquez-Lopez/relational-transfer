### What survived a deliberate attempt to break the context hypothesis

The useful variable is **task-conditioned comparison pressure**, together with the state reached during optimization. Static support-graph distinguishability and marginal exposure are incomplete proxies. The A and E graphs give a suggestive contrast: their missing pair 1–2 has the same degree, triangle count, and no directly queried 1–2 relation, but different neighborhoods; E typically learns a strong usable contrast and A does not. An exhaustive enumeration of the 455 ways to omit three edges from the six-node complete graph found only two focal graph classes satisfying the same degree-3, triangle-count-3, ten-triangle constraints: A and E (`d24_summary.json`). Graph topology alone cannot provide a richer factorial test under these constraints.

I therefore changed the *comparison task* on the A graph. In F, selected S-family training rows replace a source or target foil with the corresponding identity-1-versus-2 alternative. The queried relations remain observed; no 1–2 relation becomes a queried answer. About 204,818 of 1,024,000 training examples change, introducing about 102,000 direct source and 102,000 direct target comparisons (`d25_comparison_design.json`). F1701, trained from A1701's initial state and schedule, develops final source/target query contrasts of 1.322/1.223 and withheld-pair accuracy 4,291/4,368 = 0.982 (`d26_manifest.json`, `d29_developmental.csv`). By contrast, G increases focal exposure to nearly F's overall level while retaining A's comparison sets, and H matches F's focal exposure specifically within the S family while retaining A's comparison sets. G and H end with source/target contrasts 0.034/0.049 and 0.039/0.070, and withheld-pair accuracies 0.587 and 0.573 (`d27_frequency_design.json`, `d41_s_frequency_design.json`, `d29_developmental.csv`, `d43_h_developmental.csv`). Thus neither overall exposure nor S-family exposure explains F's rescue. F's changed foil relationships are the stronger discriminator.

F is a particularly clear **task-necessity** case. On the same deterministic 8,192 training examples, A's final model has low A loss (0.009) but fails modified F rows (loss 0.462, accuracy 0.745), while F's final model fits both tasks (loss around 0.005–0.006). A's original task therefore admits at least one separated solution; collapse is not logically forced by A's labeled examples (`d31_cross_task_loss.csv`). Tying identity 1/2 inputs after F training raises F-task loss from 0.006 to 0.091 on a 4,096-example readout, while the same merger barely changes A or G loss (`d36_merger_loss.csv`). The F objective has made this input distinction materially useful.

The acquisition trajectory also matters. F and G/H begin with available query and candidate contrast. During F training, both contrasts grow and align with the source/target alternatives. During G/H training, query contrast progressively shrinks despite near-perfect familiar performance; G briefly redirects target substitutions at update 100 before suppressing them by update 500 (`d29_developmental.csv`, `d43_h_developmental.csv`). The change is an optimization outcome, not an absent initial signal. A 2,000-update A→F switch rapidly restores the distinction, while an F→A switch erodes it only partly (`d33_switch_developmental.csv`). Even after **8,000 uninterrupted A updates** from F, source/target query contrasts remain 0.955/0.721 and redirection 1.000/0.922 (`d38_long_withdrawal.csv`). This is substantial path dependence: current task constraints do not uniquely determine the learned representation.

The strongest falsification of the early interpretation is **tied E**. An E model trained for all 8,000 updates with identity-1/2 message symbols and candidate input columns projected exactly equal still attains familiar S/R joint accuracy 0.9985/1.000, essentially the untied E model's 0.9985/0.9999. Its withheld 1–2 accuracy is 0.651 instead of untied E's 0.975 (`d40_tied_e_comparison.json`). On the same 8,192 training examples, untied/tied E cross-entropies are 0.00518/0.00683, a real but small difference (`d46_tied_untied_loss.csv`). E's contextual asymmetry therefore **does not make separation necessary to solve its familiar task**. It can steer learning toward separation, yet a nearly equally successful collapsed task solution exists. Post-training identity merger worsens untied E loss more than A/G but much less than F (`d36_merger_loss.csv`), consistent with a soft rather than hard task preference.

To test whether E's continuing task can recover the distinction from an exactly tied state, I released the tie on the completed tied E checkpoint and continued for 2,000 E updates. A matched branch retained the tie. The released branch grew source/target query contrasts from exactly zero to 0.655/0.809, with shared-scene redirection 0.992/1.000 and withheld 1–2 accuracy 0.914. The retained branch remained at zero query contrast and 0.671 withheld accuracy (`d45_release_developmental.csv`, `d44_manifest.json`). The released model is still weaker on the withheld pair than the originally untied E model, so the continuation is a partial recovery, not complete convergence to the same solution.

The decisive task counterfactual began from **the same tied E checkpoint**, with a fresh optimizer and the same index/epoch schedule, but trained on A examples. After 2,000 A updates, source/target query contrasts were only 0.028/0.045, shared-scene redirection 0.609/0.281, and E-evaluated withheld 1–2 accuracy 2,891/4,368 = 0.662 (`d49_tied_e_to_a_developmental.csv`, `d48_manifest.json`). The E-versus-A task during continuation changes whether a tied distinction becomes behaviorally usable. This controls the starting weights, optimizer reset, training length, and indexed schedule; the example semantics and graph-induced contexts differ.

### What died or weakened

1. **Graph-context distinction as an identifiability theorem:** false. Tied E solves E's familiar task nearly as well as untied E. A graph label is a useful design descriptor, not a guarantee that the trained model must separate the pair.
2. **Raw exposure as the acquisition variable:** contradicted by G and the tighter H control. Neither matches F's outcome despite matching focal exposure at relevant levels.
3. **Static graph connectivity or effective resistance as a sufficient law:** contradicted by the A/E/F/G choice-graph comparison. Normalized focal effective resistance is slightly *higher* for E than A although E generalizes far better (`d30_summary.json`). Direct comparison structure and optimization matter.
4. **A unique low-loss representation for a task:** contradicted by A/F cross-task evaluation, tied E, task switches, and long F→A withdrawal. A near-flat A-task loss path between same-seed A and F final weights contains a sharp transition in unsupported target redirection (0.078 at A, 0.961 at 75% toward F) (`d34_loss_bridge.csv`, `d35_interpolation_behavior.csv`). This parameter interpolation is descriptive; it does not establish a topological basin or a specific circuit.
5. **A one-step gradient or simple scalar force as the whole mechanism:** not supported. At the tied E parent, the fixed-sample antisymmetric gradient norm under A is 0.00662, *larger* than under E (0.00310); its descent direction is also more aligned with E's first ten-step parameter split (cosine 0.423 versus −0.050). Yet repeated A updates fail to form useful contrast, while repeated E updates succeed (`d47_tied_gradient.csv`, `d49_tied_e_to_a_developmental.csv`). Later radial gradients are mixed: at E-release step 500, A strongly opposes the existing split (−0.00845), while E only weakly opposes it (−0.00110); at step 2,000, A still opposes (−0.01420) and E is near neutral (+0.00026). At A-release step 500, the local A radial component is positive despite a small behavioral contrast (`d50_radial_gradient.csv`). These are first-order readouts at selected states, not a model of AdamW's preconditioned, stochastic, coupled trajectory. The divergence involves feedback through the rest of the network and training path.

### Current explanation and its limits

The most compact account that survives is a **conditional optimization account**, with two distinct routes. Directly contrastive candidate/foil tasks (F) make identity separation valuable for training decisions and drive both encoders to preserve and align it. This suggests a sharper task-level concept than generic graph distinguishability: a **choice-set collision** occurs when conflating two identities makes rival candidates hard to rank for the same message. F creates many such cases, while A/E have no direct 1-versus-2 candidate comparisons. This is an explanatory proposal, not a proven collision theorem, because the rendered scenes also contain visual nuisance variation. Asymmetric relational contexts (E) can bias the training trajectory toward the same useful distinction even though the task remains nearly solvable with tied identities. Symmetric or merely exposure-enriched contexts (A/G/H) tend to suppress that distinction from ordinary initialization. Once a separated computation is established, it persists under A training for a long time. Thus the mapping from experience to behavior is mediated by training dynamics and history, not just support topology or final training accuracy.

This account remains **discovery-level and incomplete**. F changes multiple foil contingencies at once, and all F/G/H and tied/release controls were run at discovery seed 1701. The tied E model constrains only identity input rows, not every possible distributed representation. The continuation comparison shows a task-dependent trajectory, but it does not identify which gradient covariance, parameter interaction, architectural bias, or optimizer state produces that trajectory. No compact transformer-native operator, grammar, or compositional law has yet been isolated. Query/candidate contrast and score alignment describe the execution bottleneck; they do not by themselves explain acquisition.

The next untouched test should separate **task necessity**, **context asymmetry**, and **history** prospectively. One clean design would factorially vary direct identity foils and asymmetric neighborhoods, include tied versus untied initialization and a task-switch arm, then predict both familiar loss and unsupported pair redirection before training. Developmental checkpoints should measure whether a distinction appears first in candidate encoding, message routing, or their alignment. An optimizer comparison or full-batch training arm would test whether AdamW/minibatch dynamics are essential. These are proposals, not confirmation. P3.2-E remains the experimental endpoint.

The closest literature analogies are [feature averaging as an implicit gradient-descent bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10322) and [competing memorizing and generalizing circuits in transformer grokking](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15071). They motivate mechanisms to test; neither establishes a mechanism in this synthetic system.

## Provisional main result from D01–D23

The first mechanistic candidate was **training-context distinguishability**. A missing pair whose identities appear in the same eligible training contexts tends to lose task-useful identity contrast. A missing pair whose identities have different contexts can retain it, even with no direct queried relation between them.

This was found adaptively in A/B/C/D and then challenged with a new E graph. A's missing pair 1–2 has identical observed neighbors, `N(1)=N(2)={3,4,5}`. E's missing pair 1–2 has different neighbors, `N(1)={3,4,5}` and `N(2)={0,4,5}`. Both identities have degree 3 and participate in three eligible training triangles; the full A and E pools each have 3,840 symbolic cases and 10 triangles. Both graphs have 12 observed edges. The pair 1–2 is absent as a queried source-target relation in both.

The new E models all acquired a large contrast and high withheld-pair performance. The model-level table is `d23_focal_models.csv`:

| Focal pair 1–2 | A discovery seeds 1701–1703 | E exploratory seeds 1701, 2701–2703 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Block-0 value-projection gain, approximate mean | 0.466 | 1.063 |
| Final source-query contrast, approximate mean | 0.039 | 0.936 |
| Final target-query contrast, approximate mean | 0.059 | 1.034 |
| Withheld-pair accuracy, range | 0.578–0.582 | 0.973–0.985 |
| New shared-scene source redirection | 0.555–0.625 | 1.000 in each model |
| New shared-scene target redirection | 0.047–0.078 | 1.000 in each model |

The scene redirection comparisons use exactly the same 256 constructed candidate scenes per model, with two common observed third identities (4 and 5), both events, 32 deterministic repetitions per cell, and balanced candidate positions. E's query changes align with the candidate contrast on these scenes (mean cosine around 0.85–0.91 for source and 0.94–0.97 for target); A's source alignment is near zero and target alignment is small. The alignment describes the scorer's geometry; it is not a causal explanation by itself.

### Strongest matched control

E1701 was newly trained with the same seeded model initialization procedure as canonical A1701. Its schedule **index and epoch arrays match A1701 exactly** (asserted before training). The symbolic pools differ, so identical indices expose the models to different relation contexts. This is a paired training-randomization control, not a prospective independent test. On the exact shared scenes, A1701 source/target redirection was 0.625/0.078; E1701 was 1.000/1.000. Their source-query contrasts were 0.030/1.148, and candidate contrasts 0.334/1.097. E1701 familiar S and R joint accuracy was 0.9985/0.9999, so the effect did not come from wholesale failure to learn training cases.

### What exposure controls do and do not rule out

An exact finite-schedule audit found almost equal numbers of source, target, candidate, and triangle appearances for identities 1 and 2 in A and E. Under the matched seed, the pair never occurs together as a **queried endpoint relation**, but it can occur as an endpoint plus a distractor in a rendered scene. The latter count is almost identical: 409,606 of 4,096,000 candidate scenes in A and 409,594 in E. Individual 1 and 2 distractor counts are also close (`d22_exposure.json`). Thus simple marginal frequency or raw visual co-occurrence of the two identities is a poor explanation for the large contrast. This does **not** equalize all higher-order context statistics: their associations with other endpoints and triangles are precisely what changed.

## What the trained network appears to do

The A/B/C/D discovery subset first suggested that missing **false-twin** pairs—nonadjacent vertices with identical open neighborhoods—had low value-projection gain, final query contrast, and accuracy. D's missing pair 0–2 was in this group, while its missing 3–4 and 4–5 pairs were distinguishable and performed much better. The term “false twins” is standard graph terminology for identical open neighborhoods; see [the formal definition](https://academic.oup.com/comjnl/article/68/10/1355/8116801). This graph label is descriptive, not a theorem about what the network must represent. Within A–D, neighborhood distinguishability was confounded with degree imbalance. E specifically broke that confound for the focal pair: identities 1 and 2 have the same degree and triangle exposure but different contexts.

An alternative, more general graph descriptor is the total-variation distance between each identity's distribution of other-identity pairs in eligible training triangles. It is zero for A's 1–2, 0.667 for E's 1–2, and grades E's other missing pairs at 0.4 (`d16_pairs.csv`). Within-model rank associations are only moderate and partly reflect observed versus missing pairs. It is a candidate acquisition variable, not an established predictive law.

The learned change is active. In an exact reconstruction of seeded initial models, false-twin source-query distance fell from roughly 0.47 initially to 0.036 after training, while observed-pair distance rose from roughly 0.49 to 1.20. The block-0 value-projection gain similarly fell for false twins and rose for observed pairs (`d10_category_model.csv`). Initial identity symbols and queries were not inherently indistinguishable.

The execution path is more distributed than the early block-0 CLS story implied. For E2701 source substitution 1→2, block-0 CLS distance is only 0.057, yet final query distance is 0.840. The changed source token after block 0 differs substantially, and the second block creates the CLS contrast. For target substitution, block-0 CLS is already far apart (0.658) and final distance is 0.941. Thus there is no single stage at which all endpoint distinctions must enter CLS.

Activation patching sharpened this point and falsified the simple “changed token carries the distinction” hypothesis. For the E2701 source change, patching only the changed source token after block 0 accounts for about 0.20 of the directed final contrast, while patching the other message tokens accounts for about 0.83. Across three E seeds, source, target, event, and padding-token patches individually induce nonzero directed changes; patching all non-CLS tokens accounts for about 0.97. These fractions are **not additive** because hybrid states interact nonlinearly. Source identity information is disseminated across token positions before block 1 reads it into CLS. For the target change, patching block-0 CLS alone accounts for about 0.96 in E2701. This is a causal hybrid-state diagnostic at a specific layer, not a clean mediation proof. Work on [contextual information mixing in transformers](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.595/) supplies a useful methodological analogy, not evidence for this particular model.

The candidate encoder changes in parallel. On identical new scenes, A's initial candidate output contrast averages 0.539/0.647 for source/target alternatives and drops after training to 0.324/0.429. E2701–2703 average 0.628/0.752 initially and rise to 0.967/1.151 after training. The sharp divergence occurs mainly in the second candidate block: trained block-0 contrasts are similar across A and E for source alternatives (0.456 versus 0.448), but block-1 contrasts are 0.323 versus 0.964. This argues against explaining behavior through the message query alone. It also suggests the training context coordinates two encoders so that unseen alternatives are both separated and aligned. The current diagnostics do not identify which parameter changes are necessary or sufficient.

## What remains uncertain

1. **Discovery selection.** The false-twin/context hypothesis was found after viewing A–D outcomes. E was designed in response, and E2702/2703 were added after E2701. Their consistency is scientifically useful but does not provide independent confirmation. The matched E1701 run is also adaptive.
2. **Graph causality at the right level.** E controls several important quantities—focal degree, focal triangle count, pool size, schedule randomness, broad exposure counts—but changes other training-context structure. A graph signature may proxy for comparative training tasks, implicit regularization, or a feature of the rendering/optimization pipeline. A full factorial graph family has not been run.
3. **No global identity-collapse theorem.** A's identities 1 and 2 remain distinguishable in many observed relations and in raw input. Their final **withheld relational** contrast collapses selectively. Calling them false twins does not mean the model is forced by graph symmetry to identify them.
4. **No isolated circuit or grammar.** The value-projection gain is a useful learned marker. It is not a proven mediator. Token patching shows distributed and role-dependent routing. The candidate encoder participates. Neither a small set of operators nor a transformer-native grammar has been identified.
5. **Scoring algebra is downstream description.** Query/candidate contrast and alignment exactly affect cosine-score margins. That identity cannot by itself explain why training created those contrasts, why they are aligned, or why block 1 implements them. The earlier decision-alignment account is a good measurement layer, not yet the acquisition mechanism.
6. **P3.2 remains separate.** These diagnostics concern acquisition and execution of endpoint contrasts in the D5 family. They neither revise the prospective P3.2-E outcome nor validate trajectories, response states, operators, manifolds, “1/42,” or any other proposed ontology.

## Earlier prospective sketch (superseded by the adversarial continuation)

1. **Freeze the acquisition hypothesis in predictive form.** Specify a graph-context statistic before any new model is trained. A promising candidate is conditional distinguishability of two endpoints from the labeled distributions of *training comparisons they participate in*, including eligible triangles, candidate foils, and distractor roles. Predict both the magnitude of learned query/candidate contrast and the probability of successful redirection. Keep direct edge observation, degree, triangle count, and raw visual co-occurrence as explicit covariates.
2. **Choose new graph conditions prospectively.** Enumerate feasible graph pairs that hold focal degree, triangle exposure, global pool size, and endpoint frequencies fixed while varying context distinguishability; include a condition where the proposed statistic and simple neighborhood difference disagree if possible. Freeze the graph set, seeds, outcomes, and analysis before training. Use newly designated models or a separately authorized prospective protocol; do not touch sealed protected seeds in this discovery lineage.
3. **Test parameter and pathway necessity.** In discovery first, intervene on the second message and candidate blocks, ideally with path-specific patches and weight swaps among matched A/E models. Ask whether restoring candidate separation without query alignment, or vice versa, rescues redirection. Prefer interventions that preserve in-distribution hidden-state structure; report hybrid artifacts explicitly.
4. **Track acquisition over time.** Save checkpoints in a new exploratory lineage and measure when context signatures, token dissemination, candidate separation, and alignment emerge. Their temporal ordering would distinguish “context first, alignment later” from joint co-adaptation. Do not retrain or alter the canonical models.
5. **Search for the smallest predictive description.** Compare graph/context statistics, low-rank factorization analogies, response-disposition descriptors, and distributed nonlinear models on genuinely new graphs and intervention families. Demand out-of-condition prediction rather than post hoc fit. If a compact law fails, that is informative: the correct level may be a richer learned comparison operator rather than a small static ontology.

The practical answer from this pass is a sharper bridge to test: **training comparison contexts can preserve or erase task-useful relational contrasts, and the trained transformer may distribute those contrasts across message tokens and both sides of the scorer before behavior emerges.** That bridge is now a discovery hypothesis with unusually strong internal controls, not a confirmed general law.

## Track B update — B-D1 candidate-state transport, 2026-10-01

Track B separately tested a preregistered discovery-only candidate operation on eight permitted A/B/C/D × 1701/1703 checkpoints. The protocol, code and raw scene plan were hashed before any B-D1 forward; low-rank post-block-1 candidate-CLS maps for V (+90° rotation) and Y (highest-spare distractor) and all composed-action predictions were sealed before V²/VY/V²Y/V⁴ outcomes. See [`track_b_bd1/bd1_report.md`](track_b_bd1/bd1_report.md) and its machine-readable manifests.

**The candidate failed.** The primary V² patched-score RMSE was 1.031× identity; V²Y was 0.979× identity and 1.007× additive. Single-step V state transport barely beat identity (0.982× on validation), while Y was moderately predictive (0.834×). Predicted V⁴ drifted despite exact input and actual-state closure. V/Y predictions approximately commuted, but this did not imply correct states or scores. Only 1/8 checkpoints beat identity on both V² and V²Y, held relational-pair scenes lost the small V²Y gain, and literal cross-seed map transfer failed all four predeclared condition gates. Full-token transport did not rescue the result. The final-stage patch correctly produced the predicted scores, but because only LayerNorm followed, it did not reveal a deeper circuit.

This kills the **specified global low-rank V/Y candidate-side transport law** without changing P3.2-E or the independent Track-A acquisition work. It illustrates a general methodological trap: a small commutator and a modest single-step effect can coexist with failure under repetition, closure and downstream function. No capacity or stage rescue was attempted after the withheld outcomes.

The principal correction is that the original scene renderer permits a source-only event shortcut: an analytic source-position/velocity rule and a learned source-only classifier each reached 100% on an independently rendered balanced 9,000-scene test (B-D11). In-range counterfactual target recombinations show that trained original models are **not strictly source-only**: 18–50% of clear event-reversing recombinations flipped event preference across A/K seeds (B-D13). Target information enters the source token mainly through first-block attention values (B-D14). Yet those recombinations are outside the renderer's joint conditional support, so they do not establish in-distribution relational necessity.

To test that necessity, B-D15 trained two new explicitly exploratory listeners in a source-balanced world. Opposite-event pairs there can share exactly the same source trajectory. Seed3701 and seed3702 reached 99.92% and 99.96% independent four-choice accuracy and 99.3% and 98.8% opposite-event query accuracy on 600 paired scenes. The old single source-token value relay was weak in both. Seed3701 distributed the event change across tokens (target-token patch 41.5% flip; all non-CLS 81.3%), while seed3702 localized much more to the target token (91.3%; all non-CLS 94.2%). B-D17's frozen boundary criteria passed, but its result supports seed-dependent implementation rather than a unique operation.

B-D18 decomposed second-block attention and found substantial attention-weight participation and value×weight interaction in both balanced-world seeds. A hostile matched analysis then showed that original-world *target geometry* counterexamples also involve attention-weight changes (B-D19). The near-pure value relay in B-D6 was specific to an **identity edit**, not a world-wide computation mode. Event-preserving target geometry edits also change attention weights (B-D20), and their displacement was imperfectly matched to event-reversing edits. Thus semantic event routing cannot be isolated from generic geometry sensitivity with these scenes.

No compact cross-world relational operation or transformer-native grammar has survived the discovery tests. The strongest bounded result is **implementation flexibility under the same architecture and task, with training-dependent causal paths**; this is useful as a constraint on any proposed law, not an independent principle ready for confirmation. Further progress needs a newly designed world family that varies source-only cue availability while holding relative geometry and counterfactual support tightly matched. Additional patches on the present renderer are likely to yield diminishing discriminatory information. No language transfer is warranted yet.
