# Iteration: from path similarity to conditional relations

**17 October 2026. Exploratory reanalysis of saved traces; no new model inference.** This iteration responds to the actor-swap × voice proposal. The four earlier lexical families are exposed development examples. All new numbers below come from the existing full-dimensional arrays; reproducible code and machine results are [here](diagnose_saved_relations.py) and [here](SAVED_RELATION_DIAGNOSTICS.json).

## 1. Why unrelated paths look alike

At the final question token, full off-diagonal step-Gram correlations across four cases were 0.82 (Qwen role swaps), 0.92 (Qwen unrelated), 0.79 (Llama role swaps), and 0.90 (Llama unrelated). The shared pattern is concentrated near neighboring layer steps. Excluding entries for steps fewer than three layers apart lowers role-swap correlations to **0.32 Qwen / 0.31 Llama**, while unrelated contrasts remain **0.71 / 0.65**. Keeping only pairs at least eight steps apart leaves role-swap correlations **0.024 / −0.011**, versus unrelated **0.56 / 0.42**. These are correlations over progressively smaller, differently distributed matrix entries, so the values are not directly comparable estimators of a fixed quantity. Their qualitative asymmetry is clear: long-range role-specific turning agreement is unsupported in these four cases.

A pooled predictor using only step separation (lag) explains **22% Qwen / 41% Llama** of final-token off-diagonal Gram variation across role and unrelated curves. Thus a shared near-diagonal pattern explains *some*, not all, of the agreement. Layer identity and model-specific operation schedule probably contribute: the earlier analysis found the final block is the largest update for all role, unrelated and raw contrasts. A generic *contrast* schedule is plausible, but it has not been causally isolated as the complete explanation. In particular, subtracting a mean **raw-prompt** Gram is not a valid cure: Qwen raw-to-contrast Gram correlation is only about 0.3, and subtracting a shared template can itself create high residual correlations. Any schedule model must be fit on development families, tested on untouched families, and compared with no correction.

The same lexical case does not reliably match across models better than a different case: at the final token, matched Qwen/Llama role-Gram correlation is **0.581**, mismatched **0.573**. Among all 24 case-label permutations, three equal or exceed the observed matched mean (upper-tail fraction **0.125**). This tiny exact randomization space is only a diagnostic; it does not justify a population significance claim.

## 2. Is any task-conditioned relation visible in the saved traces?

At the *subject and object sentence sites*, the same-layer step directions under a role swap are strongly opposed in the first seven steps: mean cosine **−0.868 Qwen / −0.689 Llama**. The broad unrelated-sentence contrast is near zero (**0.011 / 0.034**). Later role-swap means remain negative: middle seven **−0.309 / −0.642**, last seven **−0.322 / −0.481**. This is a **labeled cross-site relation** that a final-token single-path Gram does not contain.

It is also exactly where the design is weakest: swapping two names between subject and object yields opposite lexical differences at those sites by construction. The unrelated control changes names without that symmetry. The observed opposition may be entirely a lexical-position artifact. It is a promising *target for a matched control*, not evidence of semantic role-binding. We cannot inspect R–V or R–RV relations at all from saved traces because these arrays contain only R. Any claim that the existing data demonstrate a linguistic atlas would be false.

## 3. Mathematical object worth testing

Use an **intervention-conditioned response graph**, with nodes for controlled transformations `R`, `V`, `RV` at declared token sites and layers. A node stores the contrast vector, update magnitude, output margin and (where feasible) selective native patch effect. An edge stores labeled cross-response inner products and the four-cell interaction. The graph is an organized set of observations, not a newly invented mathematical primitive.

For a factorial family, `I_l = h11_l − h10_l − h01_l + h00_l`. Report its norm relative to R and V, and compare `R_active` with `R_passive` and `V_A` with `V_B` across layers. The two ways of computing `I` are identical by algebra. An interaction peak is **not** evidence that the model internally performs R then V, or V then R. The [exact toy counterexamples](factorial_identifiability.py) show (a) independent binary factors have `I=0` in one code but `||I||=1` under an injective nonlinear encoding, and (b) an additive code can still drive a nonlinear output interaction. The descriptor's invariance is limited to a shared orthogonal transform, with norm ratios also tolerating global scale; it does not solve nonlinear cross-model alignment. Layerwise changes of basis would break it.

Keep **three channels** separate: geometry (what the residual stream does), selective native causal response (what a patch changes), and behavior (full-name probability and answer). Do not average them into one score. A relation graph can preserve operation labels, relative directions, magnitude, sites and depth; it can still confuse syntax with surface position and has no automatic causal interpretation. Path signatures could enrich order-sensitive descriptions, but without semantic anchors or a fixed cross-model gauge they do not resolve the fundamental operation-label ambiguity shown in the previous toy example.

The discriminating endpoint is *prediction*, not retrospective label recovery. Select a restricted mapping on development families, then ask whether it predicts an untouched family's **selective patch-effect profile** and agent/patient outcome under a new voice condition. The mapped profile must beat normalized depth, generic step norm, residual norm, output-only trajectory, single-path Gram, CKA when there are enough samples, and a surface-order/tokenization baseline. Include an ablation where transformation labels are shuffled; since labels define the graph, success at merely recovering those supplied labels does not count.

## 4. Small factorial and controls

The concrete four cells, semantic/site alignment, initial confound, and stop conditions are in [the protocol](FACTORIAL_RELATION_PROTOCOL.md). The main design fault is worth repeating: canonical active/passive voice and agent swap both reverse the two names' surface order, so their combined edit returns the original order. A raw `R×V` interaction cannot by itself distinguish semantic relation from a positional rule. Add a second frame with fixed name mentions in a preamble and referential descriptions in the event sentence, plus a position-matched lexical change; this changes the surface route by which roles are assigned. If these controls do not make the semantic and positional accounts predict different outcomes, stop before intervention work.

Use a small, fixed set of lexical families split before inspecting new outputs (for example four development and eight evaluation families, chosen and frozen in a source manifest). Include two independent verbs, participant-name pairs with matched tokenization where possible, both Agent and Patient questions, and surface-order counterbalancing. Record actual token offsets, sentence lengths and discarded families. Screen **all eight answers per family** for each model. Resolve the previous Llama readout issue with full-name sequence log probabilities and a direct check that the scorer ranks generated correct names on known examples before any factorial patching. A failed screen family stays in the denominator and receives no causal patches.

For causal outcomes, patch one predeclared site/layer range within each model, score the complete answer-string log-probability margin, and compare clean/corrupt change with self-patch, neighboring-token, unrelated-donor and same-answer/different-syntax donor controls. This distinguishes selective acquisition of the intended counterfactual answer from generic answer-state transfer. Do not choose a new site or rename an outcome after seeing evaluation families. No such factorial inference has run in this iteration.

## 5. Relationship to prior work and recommendation

[CKA](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/kornblith19a.html) already compares representation geometry across networks; a cross-response Gram alone would be close to existing kernel ideas. [DSA](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/6ac807c9b296964409b277369e55621a-Abstract-Conference.html) and [InputDSA](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/5a437d77270d5588a84d63a851a3fdeb-Abstract-Conference.html) explicitly compare dynamics and input drive in recurrent systems; heterogeneous Transformer blocks require a separate justification. [Transformer trajectory geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09287) already reports cross-layer geometric patterns. [Causal abstraction](https://www.jmlr.org/beta/papers/v26/23-0058.html) already formalizes intervention-based equivalence. Path signatures already describe ordered trajectories. [Aljaafari et al. (2026)](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1964/) report causal semantic-role circuits and developmental tracking, directly threatening any generic “first role circuit” framing. The contribution, if one appears, must be a **specific, held-out predictive relation under strong surface and schedule controls**, or a well-supported limitation of these approaches on this task. No novelty claim follows from this exploratory work.

**Decision: REVISE AND INVEST ONE SMALL GATED STUDY.** The single-path metric is falsified as a linguistic-specific readout on the exposed cases. The saved arrays reveal a labeled cross-site pattern that is non-specific under current controls. The mathematical atlas remains a coherent hypothesis, but the first factorial alone has a positional alias. The next investment is justified only if the control frame separates that alias and baseline competence passes. If the generic/positional baselines perform equally well on untouched families, reject the proposed method rather than expand the experiment.
