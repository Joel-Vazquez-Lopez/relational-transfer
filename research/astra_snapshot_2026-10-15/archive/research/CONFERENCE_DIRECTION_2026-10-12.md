# Candidate conference direction: event-role binding and answer transfer

Date: 12 October 2026. This is a research design, not a claim of novelty or success.

**13 October update:** The original logit-level transport loss failed its local screen. A new matched slot-first output-order pilot passed its frozen screen on constructed held-out combinations, and a post hoc candidate-list placebo did not match it. However, deterministic routing from roles generated by the answer-first control nearly matched slot-first direct answers. See [the full result](role_slot_routing_pilot_2026-10-12/RESULTS.md). The novelty and natural-language transfer questions remain open; the design below is historical context for these experiments.

**Later 13 October update:** A prospectively frozen second lexical bank confirmed slot-first over both answer-first and candidate-first in both starts: **106/144 and 103/144**, versus **25/144 and 36/144** and **59/144 and 46/144**, respectively. The gain still belongs only to one constructed generator and one model family. See [fresh confirmation](role_slot_routing_fresh_confirmation_2026-10-13/RESULTS.md). A post hoc [output-format crossover](role_slot_routing_format_crossover_2026-10-13/RESULTS.md) showed that the answer's position relative to generated role fields is central: when the slot-first model is asked to answer before the roles, its first-token crossed score falls to 24/144 and 19/144. Both opposite-format prompts cause substantial schema failures. This narrows the candidate contribution to **role-to-answer use under a trained output interface**, pending natural-language and cross-family tests. Prior work has already used [semantic-role supervision for generalization](https://aclanthology.org/2020.repl4nlp-1.11/) and [semantic roles for question answering](https://aclanthology.org/D07-1002/); neither role supervision nor intermediate roles can be claimed as a first here.

**Natural-language gate on 13 October:** The prospectively frozen [QA-SRL Gold probe](role_slot_routing_qasrl_gold_2026-10-13/RESULTS.md) **failed** to transfer the direct-answer gain. Slot-first scored 84/204 and 81/204 exact natural answers, below answer-first at 90/204 and 92/204 and candidate-first at 94/204 and 88/204; all-four-correct items were 1/51 and 2/51. A second-candidate bias appeared in all arms. Post hoc routing from slot-first's generated role fields improved to 112/204 and 109/204, which points to an answer-use gap but is still far from robust and uses a known question-role mapping. The natural labels are human QA annotations, while our actor/patient mapping is automatic and not student-reviewed. This is a boundary of the current method, not a conference-level natural-language repair. A new intervention needs disjoint natural training and a fresh natural confirmation set.

**Natural verifier pilot on 13 October:** The frozen [five-arm natural-role development comparison](natural_role_verifier_development_2026-10-13/RESULTS.md) tested answer-only, candidate-first, role-first, clean-draft verification, and noisy-draft verification on 40 new source-development items in two starts. The noisy-draft *learned* repair **failed its prespecified positive screen**: 2/40 fully correct items in each start versus 9/40 and 5/40 for clean verification. Its intermediate role fields *did* withstand deliberately swapped drafts (117/160 and 122/160 routed-correct), while clean verification's fields collapsed (36/160 and 32/160). Noisy verification's final answers under the same swap remained only 75/160 and 76/160 correct. Separately, explicit deterministic routing from the role-first model's generated fields gave a promising **system-level result**: 24/40 and 26/40 fully correct development items, versus 4/40 and 0/40 for answer-only. This uses the externally known question role and automatically mapped data; it is not independent confirmation of natural-language generalization. The previously examined 51 Gold items show the same qualitative gap but are exploratory. A 60-item test queue is sealed for independent human review and has not been run through the model.

## Question and empirical basis

Can a language model learn a stable binding between event participants and their semantic roles, then use that binding when a sentence changes form or a question changes wording? A correct answer to one prompt is weak evidence: our local Qwen tests show large prompt sensitivity, and the constructed-language fine-tuning experiments show a trade-off between familiar forms and patient-fronted clefts. Controlled Transformer results show that training relations and ordered-role supervision affect transfer. The LLM bridge remains incomplete, especially on natural language.

The latest diagnosis narrows the target. On a constructed 36-event bank, two prior checkpoints selected the correct `(actor, patient)` tuple for 21–26/36 object clefts, versus 27/36 active sentences. Sentence-omitted controls were 18/36. Yet direct patient answers on object clefts were often wrong, including on training events. Doubling object-cleft exposure at a fixed answer-target budget did not repair broad transfer. These observations motivate a *binding-to-answer* intervention, but cannot establish an internal mechanism.

## Method to test, provisionally called event-role transport

Represent the same event as two explicit slots: `ACTOR = A`, `PATIENT = B`. Train the model to fill these slots from a sentence before relying on a question's wording. Add three testable constraints:

1. **Meaning-preserving transport.** Active, passive, and selected cleft renderings of the same event should give the same ordered role slots.
2. **Role-reversal transport.** Exchanging who acts on whom should exchange the two slots. This prevents a constant-name or generic-agreement solution.
3. **Answer grounding.** For a question about the actor or patient, the direct answer distribution should match the relevant slot distribution, including under unseen form × question combinations.

The auxiliary loss would be ordinary answer cross-entropy plus slot-label cross-entropy, a symmetric divergence across meaning-preserving renderings, a slot-swapped divergence across role-reversed events, and a divergence between the requested slot and direct answer. The last term is the new causal *training hypothesis*: if the model can identify roles in a structured format but fails on direct questions, tying answers to slots should reduce that gap. The proposed losses are mechanisms to compare, not assumptions about how the pretrained model already reasons.

## Decisive comparison

Use the same pretrained checkpoint, events, answer labels, forms, question wordings, number of optimizer updates, and trainable parameter count. Log token and compute budgets, then match them as closely as possible. Run at least three paired starts for confirmation.

| Arm | What it tests |
|---|---|
| Ordinary QA fine-tuning | Base performance under the same data budget. |
| Hard-form oversampling | Whether merely seeing object clefts more often is enough. A two-start negative pilot already exists. |
| Prior role/prompt coupling | Strong local comparator using answer consistency and role contrast. |
| QA plus explicit slots | Benefit of additional role supervision without the transport constraints. |
| QA plus slots and transport | Whether the full proposed constraint adds transfer beyond the same labels and prompts. |
| Optional generic consistency | Whether agreement alone can mimic the role-sensitive method. |

Train on reversible verbs with both directions of each event. Hold out **crossed** combinations of construction and question wording, new participant pairs, and new verbs. Keep all examples from an event family in one split, so paraphrases cannot leak into test. Freeze the splits, prompts, primary metric, and exclusions before training. Analyze all starts and all forms, including failures.

Primary metric: proportion of held-out event-form groups for which **both** actor and patient answers are correct under held-out question wording. Secondary metrics: worst-form accuracy, all-wordings-correct, passive/cleft-specific performance, role-reversal correctness, direct-versus-slot gap, invalid responses, and retained accuracy on familiar forms. Report paired differences with event-family bootstrap intervals; starts are paired replicates, not independent test items.

A credible positive result needs a consistent gain over *QA plus explicit slots* and prior role/prompt coupling without a large familiar-form regression. A stronger conference claim also needs a second model family or scale, a human-verified natural challenge set, and ablations of each constraint. Existing provisional EWT/QA-SRL labels must not count as gold until independently reviewed. A negative result would still delimit the mechanism but would not support a training-improvement claim.

## What could be novel, conditionally

The candidate contribution is not semantic-role labeling, consistency training, counterfactual reversal, or syntactic transformation alone. Each has prior work. The potentially new result is a **matched, role-sensitive transport test** that connects (a) controlled relational transfer, (b) stable role slots under equivalent syntax, (c) swapped slots under participant reversal, and (d) grounded answers to unseen questions, with strong exposure and extra-supervision controls. The novelty claim must be revised after a broader literature search and after the decisive comparison succeeds.

Nearest verified literature to compare directly:

- Elazar et al. (2021), [Measuring and Improving Consistency in Pretrained Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.60/): factual paraphrase consistency; establish why invariance alone is old.
- Guo et al. (2024), [Semantic-aware Permutation Training](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.680/): mitigates reversal failures by permuting semantic units; distinguish factual reversal from event-participant role reversal.
- Jang and Lukasiewicz (2023), [Learning Conceptual Roles from Dictionary](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.527/): improves several kinds of consistency with conceptual-role relations; distinguish dictionary roles from sentence-level event participants.
- Stengel-Eskin and Van Durme (2022), [The Curious Case of Control](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.760/): role-sensitive model behavior can follow positional heuristics; motivates order controls.
- Lindemann et al. (2024), [Strengthening Structural Inductive Biases by Pre-training to Perform Syntactic Transformations](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.645/): relevant syntactic-transformation training comparator; our proposed endpoint is role answers under crossed construction/question shifts.
- Li et al. (2020), [Structured Tuning for Semantic Role Labeling](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.744/): structured loss for semantic-role labeling is established; the proposed loss itself cannot be advertised as a first.
- Raghav and Jana (2025), [Are LLMs Good for Semantic Role Labeling via Question Answering?](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-srw.21/): direct LLM QA-SRL evaluation exists; a natural-language result would need stronger controlled comparisons and role-sensitive transformations rather than simply asking who did what.

## Paper trajectory

The course paper can make a defensible narrower contribution: show the controlled transfer effect, the limits of existing role training on LLM question/form robustness, and a well-controlled pilot of the binding-to-answer idea. A conference submission needs the method to beat matched alternatives on genuinely held-out compositions and verified natural data. Do not promise this outcome in the proposal or claim that the current constructed pilots already achieve it.
