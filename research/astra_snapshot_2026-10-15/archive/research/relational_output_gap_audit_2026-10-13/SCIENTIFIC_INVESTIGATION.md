# When event roles and final answers disagree

**Scientific investigation, 13 October 2026.** The initial report audited saved experiments and primary literature; two no-training development diagnostics were subsequently added and are identified below. The 60-item sealed review set and the older 32-pair active/passive challenge were not opened or evaluated. The initial item-level calculations in `AUDIT.json` are **post hoc descriptive analysis** of existing outputs; they do not change any frozen score or decision rule.

## Executive judgment

The strongest current observation is behavioral: on natural QA-SRL sentences, the same Qwen2.5-0.5B checkpoint often emits actor/patient fields that yield the annotated answer when an external rule selects the requested field, yet its own final `Answer` field differs. This is not evidence that a stable internal semantic-role representation was present and then ignored. In particular, the external rule receives a **metadata-derived requested-role label** that the model does not receive. The model may be failing to interpret the question, to use its preceding output fields, or both.

On 40 sentence-disjoint development items, role-first direct answers are 87/160 and 85/160 correct in two starts. Routing from the *same outputs* scores 119/160 and 121/160; the stricter all-four-items score changes 1/40→24/40 and 1/40→26/40. This is a promising system diagnostic, not a validated natural-language repair. The prespecified noisy-draft verifier improvement **failed** in both starts. Its role fields resist swapped drafts, but its final answers remain weak. The 51 previously examined Gold items repeat much of this pattern but are exploratory. All natural actor/patient mappings need independent human review.

A subsequently frozen development diagnostic on **40 further, nonsealed items** found that an explicit requested-role tag did **not** pass its predeclared improvement screen. It lowered repeated actor/patient answers, but also degraded generated role fields. In a separately frozen *adaptive* follow-up, holding those fields fixed revealed a narrower positive effect: the tag raised final-answer accuracy from 88→97/160 and 85→98/160, while swapping the visible fields made it fall. This localizes part of the behavior to the interaction between question cue, printed fields, and final answer; it does **not** establish a repair or an internal role circuit. [Tag diagnostic](../question_role_tag_diagnostic_2026-10-13/RESULTS.md); [fixed-field follow-up](../fixed_fields_answer_probe_2026-10-13/RESULTS.md).

The controlled and LLM experiments share a concern about actor/patient transfer, but they use different tasks, models, targets, and measurements. Their results form a research progression, not a single demonstrated mechanism. The natural verifier's 40 items are development data from the same source corpus as its 160 training items; they are sentence-disjoint, not participant-disjoint. The 51 Gold items were already examined before this pilot and cannot be promoted to confirmation.

## 2. Same-output paired audit: what routing really recovers

`AUDIT.json` recomputes exact-span correctness from the saved raw `role_first` outputs and checks protocol/checkpoint hashes. Each prompt has a model-generated `Actor`, `Patient`, and `Answer`. “Routed” selects the generated field named by the **metadata-derived** question role. Its comparison with the generated answer is paired on *the identical prompt and output*, avoiding a between-prompt explanation for the difference.

| Development, 160 prompts | Routed correct, direct wrong | Direct correct, routed wrong | Both correct | Both wrong |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| fresh1 role-first | **52** | 20 | 67 | 21 |
| fresh3 role-first | **58** | 22 | 63 | 17 |

The net gain is +32 and +36 prompts. At item level, routing rescues **24/40** and **26/40** items that direct generation fails to get all four correct; direct generation alone rescues only 1/40 in each start. The two starts reuse the same items, so they are not 80 independent natural examples. On the previously examined Gold set, routing rescues 28/51 and 26/51 all-four items, with the same caveat.

For development prompts with a correct requested role field but a wrong final answer, the answer equals the **other** generated role field in **52/52** and **58/58** cases. This is more specific than generic malformed output. For example, given “I support my brother” and “Who supports someone?”, one saved output has `Actor: I; Patient: my brother; Answer: my brother`. Another item reverses the relation: for “English troops led by Colonel Richard Nicolls,” the answer can be correct while the emitted actor/patient fields are reversed. Both directions of mismatch matter: emitted fields are not guaranteed to mediate the answer.

Question sensitivity is particularly weak. For each development item and candidate order, the model answered one actor and one patient question. It gave the **same final answer** to both in **69/80** question pairs in fresh1 and **67/80** in fresh3, while its actor/patient fields were unchanged across the two questions in **78/80** pairs in each start. In the already examined Gold set, identical answers occurred in 94/102 and 89/102 question pairs. A correct pair of fields plus a repeated final answer is consistent with a failure to select the requested role, but cannot by itself separate question interpretation from field use. The identical answer is not simply always the first or second candidate: among fresh1's 69 same-answer development pairs, it matches the first candidate 31 times and the second 37 times; one matched neither.

The constructed fresh lexical bank independently shows a related *behavioral* contrast: on 144 crossed cases, answer-first fields could be routed to **98/144 and 99/144** correct actor+patient pairs, while its own direct answers achieved **25/144 and 36/144**. In the post hoc paired audit, 73 and 63 cases were routed-correct/direct-wrong. After slot-first training, those discordant counts fall to 10 and 9, with direct scores 106/144 and 103/144. This supports the importance of the trained output interface in constructed language, but the natural failure shows that interface alone does not solve the full task.

### What the router knows that the model does not

The model input includes the sentence, two candidate spans, and a natural QA-SRL question. It generates the role fields *before* its final answer, so those text fields are in its own generation context. The router reads the same generated fields **plus an external metadata-derived label saying whether the question requests actor or patient**, then applies an exact deterministic choice. The model must infer the requested role from wording such as “Who supports someone?” versus “Who does someone support?” and must produce the final span itself. The router also avoids answer-generation errors and has no uncertainty about the selector. Therefore routed-minus-direct gain is **not** a pure measure of failure to attend to the preceding role fields. An end-to-end system would need a separately evaluated question-role recognizer, or an explicit role tag supplied equally to all compared methods.

## 3. Failure taxonomy and competing explanations

| Failure location or explanation | Current evidence | Decisive distinguishing observation |
|---|---|---|
| **Event-role extraction fails** | Routed fields are wrong on 41/160 and 39/160 development prompts in role-first. Natural source sentences can be syntactically complex. | Human-reviewed roles and no-candidate span extraction reveal whether wrong fields reflect genuine event parsing errors. |
| **Question-to-role interpretation fails** | Same final answer to actor/patient questions on 69/80 and 67/80 pairs despite mostly stable fields. QA-SRL question forms are specialized and can sound unnatural. The router has an oracle role label. | Supply an explicit `Requested role: ACTOR/PATIENT` tag while keeping fields and candidates fixed. If direct answers approach routing, question interpretation was a major bottleneck. Test a separate role classifier on unseen wordings. |
| **Answer-field conditioning or output objective fails** | Correct requested field/wrong answer occurs 52 and 58 times; noisy-draft training keeps fields robust to swaps while answers stay poor. | With explicit requested-role tags held fixed, edit only the visible role fields before answer generation and measure answer changes and correctness. Compare answer distributions as well as exact spans. If answers still ignore correct fields, the learned answer interface is weak. |
| **Generated fields are a scaffold or post hoc text** | Clean verifier can give correct answers with wrong emitted fields; output-format crossover causes schema failures. Visible fields alone do not reveal hidden computation. | Field-edit and field-removal interventions; matched fields generated without the question; internal causal tests only after a behavioral dependence is established. No claim about stable internal roles from emitted text alone. |
| **Candidate/format shortcut** | Older Gold probe strongly preferred candidate two. Current role-first outputs often give the same answer across questions; candidate-order effects persist but do not reduce to one fixed position. Synthetic format crossover is brittle. | Cross candidate order with a no-candidate exact-span condition and a neutral dummy-structure control; score both roles jointly, invalid outputs, and per-order changes. |
| **Label or task artifact** | QA-SRL question spans have human judgments, but conversion to semantic actor/patient is automatic. At least one complex saved sentence raises interpretive doubts about which event and agent are being evaluated. | Blind human review of predicate, role spans, ambiguity, voice, and agentivity before model scoring. Separate grammatical subject/object from semantic agent/patient. Exclude or report ambiguous cases by a frozen rule. |
| **Synthetic-to-natural transfer failure** | Slot-first confirmation passes on constructed crossed forms but fails natural QA-SRL direct answers. | Test fresh human-verified active/passive and reversible natural events with controlled lexical overlap, then a second source and model family. |

The current data do not rank these explanations uniquely. The first two are especially confounded because metadata supplies the router's role label. The noisy-draft result narrows one path: robust *printed* role fields are insufficient for good final answers under this training objective. The newer fixed-field continuation shows that a role cue can help select printed fields when the fields are held fixed, but the full-generation tag changes and degrades the fields themselves. It does not demonstrate an internal mechanism.

## 4. Closest primary literature and novelty boundary

| Research strand | Verified prior work | Consequence for this project |
|---|---|---|
| Compositional role transfer | [Kim and Linzen's COGS](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.731/) shows a familiar-versus-systematic-generalization gap in semantic interpretation. | Novelty cannot be “models fail on unseen combinations.” Our controlled graph manipulation and paired role-to-answer measurement must do additional work. |
| Semantic roles for QA and LLM SRL | [Shen and Lapata](https://aclanthology.org/D07-1002/) use semantic roles for answer extraction; [Raghav and Jana](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-srw.21/) evaluate several LLMs on SRL via QA. [QA-SRL Bank 2.0](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1191/) supplies the annotation formalism. | Structured role prediction plus a selector is established in spirit. A rule-based selector alone is not a novel method. Our exact labels also need semantic audit. |
| Intermediate-output faithfulness | [Turpin et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/ed3fea9033a80fea1376299fa7863f4a-Abstract.html) show that plausible generated reasoning can misrepresent determinants of an answer. [Siegel et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-short.49/) distinguish output agreement from counterfactual dependence, and [Kawabata and Sugawara](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.905/) show why answer correctness alone can hide flawed rationales. | Intermediate/final mismatch is not a first discovery. We should test *role-specific dependence* with controlled edits rather than label printed fields as internal reasoning. |
| Semantic faithfulness and internal roles | [Chaturvedi et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.cl-1.5/) test whether QA tracks semantic input interventions, including predicate–argument structure. [Aljaafari et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1964/) study causally functional semantic-role circuits. | Claims about LLM semantics and role circuits have close precedents. Our emitted-field results are a different, weaker evidence type; internal claims would require new causal controls. |
| Evaluation shortcuts | [Pezeshkpour and Hruschka](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.130/) document option-order sensitivity. | Both orders, question wording, no-candidate answers, and joint event metrics are essential. Candidate bias is not a novel finding by itself. |

This is a targeted review of primary papers, not an exhaustive novelty search. I did **not** verify a paper with this exact conjunction of matched relational training, actor/patient field-versus-answer discordance, metadata-aware versus end-to-end routing, and natural construction controls. That absence in a targeted search is **not proof that no such paper exists**. A strong eventual claim would be a **causal, role-specific account of where relational QA breaks across syntax and question changes**, replicated on human-verified natural data and another model family. Current evidence supports the question and a valuable measurement framework; it does not yet support that conference-level conclusion.

## 5. Ranked next decisive experiments

**1 — Resolve the now-observed cue/field interaction on human-reviewed examples.** The new tag diagnostic and fixed-field follow-up completed the cheap no-training screen, but their automatic actor/patient mappings are provisional. Blindly verify predicate, participant, role, ambiguity, and voice for the new development bank; record exclusions before re-scoring. Then freeze a genuinely independent, balanced natural challenge with question paraphrases and both candidate orders. Preserve full generation and fixed-field continuation as distinct conditions. An explicit role tag alone failed the full-generation screen; the follow-up indicates that incorrect printed fields can become more influential. A separately evaluated question-role recognizer is needed before treating a metadata-aware router as an end-to-end system.

**2 — Extend visible-field dependence with better controls.** The exploratory cached-field swap already changed 36–50% of final answers across starts/cue conditions, demonstrating behavioral sensitivity to printed fields. On a predeclared, human-verified subset, add matched wrong-name pairs and no-field controls, along with log-probability changes and question paraphrases. Keep the output schema equally natural across conditions. This tests whether visible fields *cause* answer behavior more cleanly; it still does not prove that the same computation operates during free generation.

**3 — Independent natural robustness confirmation.** After human review, freeze a balanced challenge with active/passive alternations, reversible events, varied actor position, multiple question wordings, and no-candidate span scoring. Use the existing 60-item queue only after labels and exclusions are finalized; it is mostly direct-object material, so add a separately frozen passive/construction set. Compare untouched, answer-only, role-first direct, routing with a tested selector, and simple syntax/position baselines on the exact same examples. Add a second model family. Report event-level all-variants-correct, exact actor/patient, field correctness, routing consistency, invalids, and per-construction results. The final set remains closed until protocol lock.

**4 — Only then test a learned answer-routing intervention.** If (1) and (2) show a residual answer-use failure, compare role-first plus an explicit answer-from-field objective against matched role-first, clean verifier, noisy verifier, candidate-first, and deterministic routing. Match training examples, target tokens, updates, and as much inference compute as possible; prespecify held-out participants, verbs, constructions, and question forms. The failed noisy-draft screen remains in the record. A learned method must improve gold-answer accuracy, not merely agree with its own wrong fields.

**5 — Internal mechanism study, conditional.** If a robust behavior survives independent natural confirmation, design matched same-sentence activation or attention interventions that separate actor/patient role from participant position and question wording, extending the controlled-Transformer mechanism tests. Do not infer a Qwen mechanism from the existing printed fields or the active-only Qwen patch.

## Paper direction

The most coherent present paper question is **when extracted event roles do or do not support the answer a model gives, and how evaluation format changes that diagnosis**. The controlled Transformer study supplies motivation and a successful role-supervision comparison; the LLM studies show a constructed success, a natural transfer failure, and a more precise field/answer disagreement. That is one trajectory if the bridge is framed as a sequence of tests, not as proof that the same mechanism crosses architectures.

For a course paper, the **scientific diagnosis (A)** is currently stronger than the **system intervention (B)**. The latter is a strong baseline and diagnostic: routing performs well on development items, but it uses a gold role selector and has not survived independent reviewed testing. A stronger conference paper would need experiment 1's localization, experiment 2's causal behavioral evidence, experiment 3's robust cross-model natural confirmation, and a clear distinction from existing SRL-for-QA and faithfulness research. The failed noisy-draft repair belongs in the results, not in a footnote.
