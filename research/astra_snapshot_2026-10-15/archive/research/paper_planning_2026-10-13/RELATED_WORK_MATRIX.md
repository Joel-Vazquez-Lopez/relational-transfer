# Targeted related-work matrix

**Verified against the linked primary paper pages or author manuscripts on 13 October 2026.** This is a targeted novelty audit across ACL/EMNLP/NAACL/Findings, ICLR, and arXiv; it is not proof that no closer paper exists. “Our possible difference” is a testable position, **not an established first**. A final manuscript needs full-paper reading and citation checks.

## Relational and compositional generalization

| Verified source | Actual contribution and overlap | Our possible difference and required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Kim & Linzen, [COGS](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.731/) (EMNLP 2020) | Semantic-parsing benchmark with systematic new combinations; ordinary test accuracy can hide generalization failure. | Our matched graph rewiring changes *training relations for the same withheld pair* and tests ordered-role rescue. We cannot claim to invent compositional role evaluation. |
| Bogin et al., [Unobserved Local Structures Make Compositional Generalization Hard](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.175/) (EMNLP 2022) | Instance difficulty correlates with local structures absent from semantic-parsing training; local structure guides splits and data selection. **Close precedent** for an evidence-structure explanation. | Our graph-specific contrast is causal at the rewiring level, with degree/triangle/sham controls and a role-supervision interaction. Need ablations that distinguish overlap from other rewiring changes and natural-language relevance. |
| Zhou et al., [Data Factors for Better Compositional Generalization](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.898/) (EMNLP 2023) | Studies scale, diversity, complexity and repetition of data for compositional generalization. | We match exposure volume/degree and manipulate relation topology. This is a narrower causal factor, not a general data-selection theory. |
| Fu & Frank, [The Mystery of Compositional Generalization in Graph-based Generative Commonsense Reasoning](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.492/) (Findings EMNLP 2024) | Tests seven LLMs on unseen relation-type combinations in reasoning graphs and relates graph structure to difficulty; an easy-to-hard demonstration order helps. **Close graph/LLM precedent.** | Our graph is the *training co-occurrence network* for a fixed unseen actor–patient pair, with prospective matched rewiring and supervision comparison. Their result means graph structure plus LLM compositionality is not new by itself. |
| Sinha et al., [CLUTRR: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Inductive Reasoning from Text](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1458/) (EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019) | Semi-synthetic family stories test inference of an unmentioned relation between entities, with held-out rule compositions and noise. **Close text/graph precedent.** | Our withheld pair is an actor/patient *binding* problem, and the causal variable is the graph of examples seen during training. A natural-language bridge must distinguish itself from simply rerunning CLUTRR-style relation inference. |
| Wold et al., [Compositional Generalization with Grounded Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.205/) (Findings ACL 2024) | Generates natural-language questions paired with knowledge graphs to test new compositions while limiting answers known from pretraining. | Our planned linguistic bridge needs similarly careful contamination and grounding controls; simply adding natural-language questions to a graph task would overlap. |
| Russin et al., [Compositional Generalization by Factorizing Alignment and Translation](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-srw.42/) (ACL SRW 2020) | Separation of alignment and translation improves SCAN generalization without extra supervision. | Our ordered-role auxiliary is distinct from their architecture, but their result rules out “factorizing relational computation” as new in itself. |
| Jabbar et al., [Distinguishing fair from unfair compositional generalization tasks](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1133/) (Findings EMNLP 2025) | Some COGS failures reflect ambiguous/contradictory targets rather than inability. | Our generator audit and solvability baseline must show the withheld question has a recoverable target; otherwise graph effects can be artifacts. |
| Kumon & Yanaka, [Analyzing the Inner Workings of Transformers in Compositional Generalization](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.432/) (NAACL 2025) | Finds generalizing Transformer subnetwork and causally analyzes syntactic-feature use; also finds a noncompositional component. | Our scene-state role-direction swap adds a different *training intervention plus state test*. This paper blocks any claim that causal internal analysis of compositional generalization is unprecedented. |

## Semantic roles, LLM evaluation, and structured readout

| Verified source | Actual contribution and overlap | Our possible difference and required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Denning et al., [Do Language Models Know Who Did What to Whom?](https://doi.org/10.1162/OPMI.a.365) (*Open Mind* 2026, 10:923–950; [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13379307/)) | Tests thematic-role sensitivity across constructions and representational measures. Published title differs from the arXiv title “Do Large Language Models know who did what to whom?” | Our matched *training-distribution* and answer-routing interventions differ from observational representational tests. We must not title the paper as though role evaluation itself is new. |
| Elazar et al., [Measuring and Improving Consistency in Pretrained Language Models](https://direct.mit.edu/tacl/article/doi/10.1162/tacl_a_00410/107384/Measuring-and-Improving-Consistency-in-Pretrained) (TACL 2021) | ParaRel tests paraphrase consistency of factual relations and proposes a training method; alternations include active/passive voice and converse substitution. | Meaning-preserving consistency training is established. Our proposed role-swap comparison must measure *correct* equivariance and contrast ordered-role labels, rather than claim that consistency itself is new. |
| Aljaafari et al., [Emergence and Localisation of Semantic Role Circuits in LLMs](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1964/) (Findings ACL 2026) | Causal-temporal circuit study using contrastive minimal pairs, edge attribution and training tracking. **Close precedent** for internal role-mechanism claims. | Our controlled scene-state patch does not establish a novel LLM circuit. A conference LLM mechanism paper would need cross-form causal interventions beyond first/last-position controls. |
| Raghav & Jana, [Are LLMs Good for Semantic Role Labeling via Question Answering?](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-srw.21/) (IJCNLP-AACL SRW 2025) | Tests five LLMs on QA-SRL zero/few-shot and analyzes errors. | Our fine-tuning comparisons and separation of extraction, question classification and answer routing could add something, but require independently checked labels and stronger models. Simply evaluating Qwen on QA-SRL is not new. |
| Wang et al., [An MRC Framework for Semantic Role Labeling](https://aclanthology.org/2022.coling-1.191/) (COLING 2022) | Conditions argument labeling on predicate senses and semantic-role queries. | Typed `ACTOR/PATIENT` query conditioning is **not** a new method. Potential difference: matched diagnosis of final-answer routing versus role-field extraction in a generative LLM. Need beat role-query baselines on external data. |
| Zhang et al., [Transfer Learning from SRL to Event Argument Extraction with Template-based Slot Querying](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.169/) (EMNLP 2022) | Unifies several role-query strategies and shows template slot querying transfers effectively. | Our method needs a stronger outcome than “role query helps”; compare against template queries, direct extraction, and equivalent supervision. |
| Mehta et al., [Promptly Predicting Structures: The Return of Inference](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.7/) (NAACL 2024) | Uses structural constraints and combinatorial inference to improve prompted linguistic predictions. | Deterministic routing from generated fields is not conceptually new. A learned method must match or beat a transparent structural readout, including when fields are wrong. |
| Li et al., [LLMs Can Also Do Well! Breaking Barriers in Semantic Role Labeling via Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1189/) (Findings ACL 2025) | Studies LLM methods for SRL at stronger scale; relevant performance comparator. | Our 0.5B candidate-supported task cannot claim state of the art in SRL. We need compare on standard gold evaluation and separate our specific error-chain question. |
| Stengel-Eskin & Van Durme, [The Curious Case of Control](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.760/) (EMNLP 2022) | Tests model behavior on control constructions and semantic proto-role properties; shows language-model role heuristics matter. | Supports explicit positional and grammatical-role controls in our linguistic evaluation. |

## Faithfulness, answer use, backward planning, and procedure learning

| Verified source | Actual contribution and overlap | Our possible difference and required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Lyu et al., [Faithful Chain-of-Thought Reasoning](https://aclanthology.org/2023.ijcnlp-main.20/) (IJCNLP-AACL 2023) | LLM translates inputs to symbolic reasoning chains, with deterministic execution to make answer computation follow the chain. | External routing of generated fields is a familiar neuro-symbolic pattern. Our possible contribution is a measured extraction/routing failure chain under role and form transformations, not deterministic execution itself. |
| Mehta et al., [Promptly Predicting Structures](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.7/) (NAACL 2024) | Constrained inference from model predictions; already shows output consistency can improve structural predictions. | A comparison to constrained readout is mandatory before claiming learned answer grounding is useful. |
| [The Probabilities Also Matter](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-short.49/) (ACL 2024) | Distinguishes causal dependence on an explanation from stronger explanatory faithfulness and evaluates free-text rationales. | Swapping visible fields and seeing changed answers only shows dependence on that text, not that the fields report internal reasoning. |
| Kazemi et al., [LAMBADA](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.361/) (ACL 2023) | Goal-directed backward chaining over natural-language facts using several LLM submodules; improves deep logical proof search. | “Start from the goal” and backward reasoning are old. A new method would need learned construction and causal use of *minimal computational requirements* on held-out dependency types, not just reversed output order. |
| Zhang et al., [Reverse Chain](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.22/) (Findings NAACL 2024) | Prompt-only, target-driven backward process for multi-API planning. | Blocks broad novelty claims for reverse planning. Our proposed setting is role-sensitive linguistic relations and training, but that difference alone is insufficient. |
| [DaSLaM: Small Language Models Fine-tuned to Coordinate Larger Language Models Improve Complex Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18338) (arXiv manuscript) | Fine-tuned decomposer coordinates a larger solver; modular learned decomposition improves reasoning. | Our v5 separate clause selector plus planner is close in spirit and currently much narrower. Need genuinely novel held-out dependency compositions and matched decomposer/solver baselines. |
| Shi et al., [ExeDec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13883) (ICLR 2024 author manuscript) | Predicts execution subgoals step by step for program synthesis; tests several compositional shifts and LLM prompting. | Our goal-derived operation specification must show more than supplied clause views on two binary operations; ExeDec is a strong method and evaluation precedent. |
| Parmar et al., [PLAN-TUNING](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1087/) (EMNLP 2025) | Distills planning trajectories into smaller models via supervised/reinforcement objectives; reports reasoning and out-of-domain gains. | Training on plans is established. Distinguishing contribution would require dependency-sensitive intervention and transfer to unseen operation families with equally trained planning controls. |

## Literature decision

The **controlled graph paper** has the clearer current novelty position: a matched local-rewiring effect on one withheld role relation, attenuation by ordered-role supervision beyond participant identity, and a measured functional scene-state contribution. Its nearest threats are instance-structure/data-selection and mechanistic-compositional papers, not a paper with the exact same graph intervention found in this targeted search. That is a defensible *provisional* differentiation, not proof of first publication.

The **LLM routing paper** has greater immediate relevance to LLM evaluation, but many key ingredients already exist: role-query conditioning, structured prediction, deterministic inference, and QA-SRL evaluation. Its novel position would have to be an empirically robust *dissociation plus causal repair across candidate-free, natural, unfamiliar constructions*—currently unproven. The **Inverted CoT method** faces the densest prior work and has only a restricted feasibility result.
