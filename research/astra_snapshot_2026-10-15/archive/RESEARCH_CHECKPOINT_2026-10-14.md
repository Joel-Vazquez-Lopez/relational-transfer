## Original question and present paper ambition

The project began with a controlled question: when a model has seen two entities with other partners, can it correctly assign actor and patient roles when those entities first appear together? Graphs specify which pairs were observed during training. The broader ambition is a **pretrained-LLM and authentic-language paper** about relational role generalization, evaluation, and possibly a targeted training repair. The controlled Transformer is a causal test bed for training-evidence structure. It cannot, without direct experiments, explain how pretrained LLMs answer natural sentences.

The strongest proposed claim was: ordered-role supervision changes how dependent unseen-pair transfer is on the structure of observed training relations, **beyond** simply improving general transfer ability. The latest ability-matched development result does **not** support that stronger clause. We retain the narrower positive controlled results and every negative test.

## Controlled Transformer: established bounded results

| Study | Design and result | Evidential limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Fresh overlap confirmation](role_transfer_phase01/rq1_overlap_confirmation_v1/RESULTS.md) | Eight fresh 10-entity graph families and three paired starts. High-overlap unseen pairs were harder than low-overlap pairs under unordered identity supervision; ordered-role supervision reduced that within-graph association by **19.95 points** versus the unordered control. | An association within selected graphs; overlap is not isolated as the cause. One start lost **5.76 points** of unseen-event accuracy. |
| [Prospective rewire comparison](role_transfer_phase01/rq2_rewire_repair_v1/RESULTS.md) | Eight fresh graph families, three starts, sham/high rewire, 96 models. Unordered focal high-minus-sham penalty **−24.45 points**; ordered-role attenuation **+22.59 points**, positive in all eight families. | Rewiring changes a package of local paths/co-occurrences. It does not identify one graph property or establish an LLM effect. |
| [Phase 2 development](role_transfer_phase01/phase2_development_v1/RESULTS.md) | Five objectives, eight **already examined** graph families, two starts, 160 models, all-45-pair audit. Ordered-role versus participant-only focal attenuation **+22.57 points** at the fixed high auxiliary weight. | Ordered-role was about **10.7 points** better on other unseen pairs. Familiar accuracy was near ceiling in these controlled studies. The graph conditions hold entity degree and training count fixed as specified in their protocols, but they do not hold every local motif fixed. The focal pair is withheld from training. All model checkpoints, raw scores, protocols, graph manifests, and logs remain in their study directories.

## Controlled Transformer: failed, negative, or inconclusive tests

- The [initial 12-entity factorial](role_transfer_phase01/rq1_factorial12_v1/PHASE1_REPORT.md) and independently frozen [strict factorial](role_transfer_phase01/rq1_factorial12_strict_v1/PHASE1_STRICT_REPORT.md) both estimated a **−9.94-point** two-versus-one-common-partner difference. The strict eight-family interval was **−20.46 to +0.58**, Holm-adjusted **p=0.1563**; the frozen success criterion failed. The three-edge-path contrast and interaction were not established. Their post hoc pooled estimate is exploratory, not a replacement for the failed gates.
- The proposal's [role-swap score-consistency arm](role_transfer_phase01/phase2_development_v1/RESULTS.md) showed **+0.33-point** attenuation against its permutation compute control, with mixed family directions and descriptive **p=0.875**. It is distinct from gold ordered actor/patient supervision; we did not validate it as a repair.
- The completed [ability-matched development screen](role_transfer_phase01/ability_match_v1/RESULTS.md) calibrated four nonzero role-loss weights on nonfocal pairs, selected **0.001**, and passed a frozen **aggregate** ability gate: **85.69%** ordered-role versus **85.01%** participant-only. The focal attenuation at that operating point was **−6.10 points** (family interval **−14.70 to +2.49**, descriptive sign-flip **p=0.140625**); only two of eight families were positive. Family-specific ability gaps ranged **−4.22 to +7.57 points**. This is development on previously examined graphs. It does not prove no effect at any weight, but it gives no support to the proposed ability-independent reduction in structural sensitivity.
- We have not established a unique common-neighbor/path mechanism, a general internal neural role circuit, a successful role-swap consistency objective, or the controlled graph effect in a pretrained LLM.

**Decision:** do not present the high-weight controlled interaction as ability-independent, and do not spend a fresh confirmation cohort on that strong claim unchanged. A later controlled study would need a prespecified family-aware ability comparison or identical shared held-out pairs across graph conditions.

## Pretrained LLMs and constructed language: what was actually found

- In [Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct synthetic confirmation](llm_role_repair_confirmation/RESULTS.md), ordinary fine-tuning scored **51.51%** on withheld-pair patient-fronted clefts; ordered-role auxiliary training scored **75.53%** across three graph/name worlds and two starts (**+24.02 points**, 6/6 positive). Familiar active/passive accuracy fell from **99.98% to 97.96%**. The untouched pretrained model scored **86.49%** on the clefts, so this was partial protection against fine-tuning regression, not an improvement beyond the base model.
- The [matched participant-only auxiliary control](llm_role_unordered_control/RESULTS.md) scored **83.82%** on those clefts, exceeding the ordered-role arm at the tested weight while losing more familiar-form accuracy. The benefit cannot be attributed uniquely to ordered semantic-role labels. These Qwen studies vary training graphs across worlds but **do not** test paired sham/high evidence structures for the same focal pair, so they are not the direct LLM counterpart of the controlled interaction.
- [Qwen2.5-1.5B wording/form](llm_role_structure_v1/RESULTS.md) and [internal-state diagnostics](llm_role_internal_v1/RESULTS.md) and [same-sentence donor control](llm_role_internal_v2/RESULTS.md) found sharp question- and voice-dependent answers, with a block-20 state patch that can steer answers in selected cases. The selected cases were active/position-confounded, so this does not establish a general abstract role circuit.
- [Slot-first output training](research/role_slot_routing_fresh_confirmation_2026-10-13/RESULTS.md) passed a prospective **new-lexical-bank** constructed-language screen against answer-first and candidate-first controls. A deterministic rule routing the model's generated actor/patient fields approached or exceeded the learned final answer, suggesting an answer-routing bottleneck in this setting. It still uses synthetic templates, one model family, and a hand-known requested role. The later [natural verifier pilot](research/natural_role_verifier_development_2026-10-13/RESULTS.md) **failed** its positive learned-repair screen; correcting generated role fields did not reliably correct final answers.
- The [goal-first decomposition branch](research/goal_first_decomposition_v5_2026-10-13/RESULTS.md) produced a narrow generated-grammar feasibility result for learned clause selection and operation choice. It did not show general planning, full natural-language answering, or a conference-ready training paradigm. Keep it a separate exploratory branch unless new independent evidence connects it to this paper.

## Authentic language: results and validity limits

- On 63 selected QA-SRL Gold sentences, an [A/B answer evaluation](llm_role_authentic_transfer/RESULTS.md) had strong answer-position preference. Ordered-role auxiliary training did **not** establish a natural-sentence repair. The [direct-span follow-up](llm_role_authentic_spans/RESULTS.md) and [51-event candidate contrast](llm_role_natural_contrast_confirm/RESULTS.md) separated response-format problems from candidate-relative role discrimination, but the examples had already been inspected; they are exploratory, not new blind confirmation.
- A [natural-role continuation](llm_role_natural_repair/RESULTS.md) trained Qwen2.5-0.5B on **256 QA-SRL Bank 2.0 events**. On a **130-event document-disjoint Bank test**, candidate-relative role contrast improved **97→121/130** and **93→121/130** across two starts. Exact direct *both-role* answers improved **2→41/130** and **4→49/130**, still far from reliable. These gains combine natural syntax, role labels, answer-format practice, and optimization; they do not isolate one causal ingredient. The Bank evaluation is a recipe screen, not final blind confirmation.
- A separate [75-item UD English EWT check](llm_role_independent_eval/RESULTS.md) showed candidate-contrast gains **56→75/75** and **56→74/75**, and direct both-role exact answers **0→15/75** and **0→19/75**. A copy-practice control did not reproduce the gain. EWT nominal subject/object are **syntactic proxies**, not verified semantic actor/patient labels; the set strongly favors straightforward active, actor-first sentences. Qwen2.5-1.5B was evaluated as an untouched baseline, not as a second-model training repair.
- The [32-pair active/passive challenge](llm_role_challenge/STATUS.md) has a frozen protocol, blind review sheet, gate, and evaluator. **0/32 challenge pairs and 0/75 EWT items have independent human-verified semantic-role mappings.** The provisional AI review is a triage aid, not independent gold. No model score on the sealed 32-pair challenge exists.

## What we have **not** found or done

1. No verified ability-independent controlled structure × ordered-role effect; the latest development screen points in the opposite direction at its chosen weight.
2. No isolated single graph property causing focal role-transfer failure.
3. No matched sham/high **pretrained-LLM** experiment testing the same training-evidence-structure × objective interaction.
4. No independently human-verified, balanced authentic-language confirmation of an LLM role-answering repair across active/passive, role swaps, participant order, and question wording.
5. No successful learned natural-language role-routing/verifier method that beats simple deterministic routing under matched controls.
6. No established general internal mechanism, cross-model repair, or verified conference-level novelty. Such claims require a fresh literature audit and new evidence.

## Next scientific gate, without launching a new experiment here

The paper should keep LLMs and real language central. The immediate measurement bottleneck is independent semantic-role review of the prepared challenge, with active/passive and participant-order balance and exact actor **and** patient answers. If that confirms a failure, identify whether role extraction, question interpretation, answer routing, or syntax change causes it. Then freeze one targeted intervention with matched answer-only, participant-only, ordered-role, generic-consistency, and compute/data controls as relevant. A direct matched-network Qwen experiment remains possible, but the negative ability-matched Transformer result means it cannot be treated as a foregone bridge.

The strongest potential paper is a **single, specific LLM and real-language claim** supported by fresh reviewed items and meaningful controls. The controlled work supplies motivation and bounds; it is not sufficient to confer novelty on the LLM study.
