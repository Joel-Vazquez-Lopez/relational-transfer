## What is established, and what remains open

- **Established in the controlled task:** matched graph condition affects unseen role transfer; ordered-role supervision largely repairs that effect; internal-state interventions provide limited causal evidence in that model.
- **Established in Qwen's constructed tests:** ordinary fine-tuning can damage a noncanonical form; auxiliary heads protect it, but participant-only supervision can protect it at least as well. Therefore the controlled role-specific mechanism has **not** been transferred to Qwen.
- **Established as exploratory natural-language evidence:** natural role adaptation improves candidate discrimination and some direct generation on selected QA-SRL and syntax-proxy EWT items. A copy-only control and narrow replay-only control are negative. The result remains vulnerable to positional shortcuts, label meaning, phrasing, selection, and one-model dependence.
- **Unresolved:** whether role information is internally present but unavailable for generation; whether transformation invariance and role reversal are jointly learned; whether fine-tuning forgetting or new abstraction explains Qwen improvements; whether any offline phase helps beyond equal-data interleaving or augmentation.

## Reuse and non-repetition

Reuse saved controlled graph families and model checkpoints for code and cost calibration, but reserve **new graph families** for any new confirmation. Reuse Qwen source adapters only for diagnostics on *new development material*; never treat the already-seen Bank or EWT results as independent confirmation. Do not rerun the eight-family RQ1/RQ2 tests, participant-only versus ordered synthetic clefts, dense early stopping, narrow synthetic replay, or the existing QA-SRL A/B test as if they were unanswered questions. No claim about the 32-pair active/passive challenge until independent review completes.
