# Competing explanations and decisive predictions

Hypotheses can jointly hold. “Weakens” below means the specified simple version loses support under the specified measurement; it does not prove a universal negative. The existing reserved active/passive challenge is outside all development experiments here.

| ID | Falsifiable prediction | Smallest discriminating experiment | Serious competing explanation and negative control | Result that weakens the hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1: insufficient reusable relation | On a new graph, models trained with role-specific targets transfer more than equal-parameter, equal-update participant-identity and random-label auxiliary controls on *both* new pairs and role-reversed items. | Small Transformer; one fresh graph family for screening, then independent families for confirmation; compare ordered role, participant-only, and random-label heads under matched budgets. | Generic auxiliary regularization. Random or participant-only heads provide the same gradients/architecture without ordered roles. | Role-specific condition has no consistent advantage beyond identity/random heads on hard joint-role accuracy. |
| H2: information exists but cannot be expressed | A frozen probe trained on independent items and candidate likelihoods recover correct roles on items where direct generation fails; a position-matched state intervention shifts answers selectively. | On existing checkpoints, use a new development set with candidate scoring and direct answers; train probes on disjoint graphs and test on changed constructions. | Probe reads an unused cue or candidate priors. Controls: sentence omitted, participant order reversed, lexical-held-out probe test, equal-energy random intervention. | No robust role signal survives position and lexical controls, or direct failure is fully due to parsing/prompt format rather than role access. |
| H3: transformation failure | A model gets canonical and passive forms of an event right but fails patient-fronted clefts or true role reversals for the same event, beyond heuristics. | Source-only generated L0–L2 set with an event-level quartet: canonical, passive equivalent, cleft equivalent, actor/patient swap. | Surface rule or bad generated sentence. Controls: first/second participant, by-phrase, grammatical-subject rules; manual grammar audit of a small blind sample. | Joint correctness across forms and role swaps remains high on unseen pairs after shortcut controls. |
| H4: optimization/forgetting | Pretrained model is already good on noncanonical forms; ordinary fine-tuning reduces that score as familiar score rises; protection tracks general auxiliary regularization more than role labels. | Same pretrained checkpoint, frozen before/after curves for ordinary, ordered-role, participant-only, and random-label auxiliary arms at comparable familiar accuracy. | Training data mismatch or lower effective learning rate. Controls: matched token/update counts, learning-rate and gradient-norm logs, sampled early-stop and same-strength non-role heads. | Noncanonical failure predates fine-tuning or role-specific targets beat all general controls at equal familiar accuracy. |
| H5: insufficient structural consolidation | After identical observations and updates, an **offline** phase yields a larger held-out transfer gain than the same data/loss interleaved, with no new factual labels. | Start from the same wake checkpoint; contrast offline S2 with matched interleaved S2 plus extra-update and transformed-data controls on L2. | Data augmentation, extra gradients, schedule or learning-rate effects. Keep examples/targets/tokens/optimizer/schedule matched; change only ordering if feasible. | Offline and interleaved match within uncertainty, or gain vanishes against augmentation/compute controls. |
| H6: coverage and supervised format | Varied natural sentences or explicit answer practice explain apparent transfer better than offline role consolidation. | Factorial controls on a development set: natural role questions, copy-only questions with same spans, unlabelled natural exposure, and format-matched non-role supervision. | Different target entropy or candidate priors. Use same source sentences, answer spans, update budget, and balanced targets where possible. | Role-specific condition improves held-out voice/role-swap joint accuracy beyond all coverage and format controls. |
| H7: shortcut and measurement confounding | Aggregate candidate accuracy is high while exact both-role, both-option-order, and role-reversal correctness stay low; scores swing with wording or answer position. | Evaluate the same verified event across both option orders, free answer, two wordings, voice, cleft and true role reversal. | Gold ambiguity and span boundary. Blind human review, fixed normalization and semantic role criteria. | High event-level correctness and stable item-level likelihood margins across formats. |
| H8: train/test semantic mismatch | Syntactic subject/object scores overstate agent/patient transfer, especially for non-agentive and non-patient events. | Blindly code semantic roles on source-only EWT items before looking at model outputs; report both the original syntactic score and a separately frozen semantic subset. | Small or selected sample. Stratify by predicate and construction; abstain on disputed items. | Syntactic and verified semantic scores agree closely across constructions and models. |

## Current weight of evidence

The **best-supported immediate explanation for the Qwen synthetic pattern is H4 plus H6**: ordinary fine-tuning reduces a pretrained patient-first ability; auxiliary training preserves it, and participant-only targets beat ordered-role targets on that test. This is an inference from saved results, not an isolated causal finding. H5 remains interesting but currently lacks a positive result and must compete directly with ordinary replay, augmentation, and interleaving. H2 and H7 are especially relevant to the authentic-language gap: candidate discrimination can be high while direct both-role generation is low, and A/B position is a documented confound.

## Decision priority

1. Finish an **independently human-verified** natural test without touching reserved labels during design. If the reserved challenge remains pending, use a separate development corpus or wait; do not silently substitute AI review for human review.
2. Implement a deterministic L0–L2 ladder and show it defeats the easy heuristics. This is a validity gate, not a training success.
3. Use a tiny, predeclared H5 schedule contrast only if the benchmark has headroom and leakage checks pass. It should test *offline versus interleaved with identical information* rather than “sleep versus nothing.”

An accuracy rise cannot alone decide H1 versus H2 versus H4. A mechanistic follow-up needs independently generalizing probes and causal interventions with position and random-subspace controls.
