# Why the investigation continues

The user explicitly corrected Codex's decision to end the broader task at the previous pilot stop. This is a new authorized investigation, with a changed estimand frozen before fresh outcomes. Prior failed gates remain failed; old held families remain untouched. We do not erase start markers, resume the old run, retroactively calibrate its correctness, or claim its carrier hypothesis was tested.

The design error was treating perfect uncalibrated binary choices as a universal prerequisite for measuring graded role/query dependence. Such a requirement is appropriate for some correct-answer mechanism claims but can prevent studying erroneous processing or systematic graded sensitivity. This study reports all three quantities separately: native choices, one independently fixed calibrated classifier, and bias-cancelling factorial response. A positive score contrast is not native answer competence or a uniquely identified semantic variable.

Before any real target-model call or active protocol commit, a final design review added the explicit question-workspace alternative to the two carrier alternatives. The paired masks can show that earlier question states suffice even when neither period route contributes. Omitting a prospective criterion for that already stated competitor would leave an avoidable interpretation gap. The first local draft lock is preserved as DRAFT_LOCK_BEFORE_SUFFIX_REVIEW.json; the final lock and full synthetic rehearsal are regenerated before outcomes. The earlier unreferenced Git tree is not an active published protocol. No hypothesis or criterion is changed after target outcomes.

All standard prompt wording, source masks, class aliases, fixed-shape/native computation and numerical tolerances are retained. New name/verb combinations are chosen without model feedback. Two development and four evaluation families allow a small prospective generalization test. Both models get the full fixed panel regardless of scientific positivity. Only technical invalidity/resources stop collection. There is no new prompt/model search or variable outcome-dependent budget.

The frozen readout threshold uses only the old exposed d1 scores. Native decision failures are never hidden. The factorial endpoint is a standard difference in differences; no mathematical novelty is claimed. All four R×Q cells and both voices are necessary. First-mentioned-only copying predicts opposite role-oriented signs across voices, while a syntax-aware copier can still reproduce correct signs. This test cannot distinguish all possible implementations of linguistic meaning.

The native engine is copied from the prior validated engine with embedding capture added; its model operations and attention masks are unchanged. Prefix/suffix control tensors are hashed, selected period/end residual traces and full logits are retained losslessly. Every stored16bit value must exactly round-trip to the original32bit capture; otherwise use32bit. This storage policy changes no inference arithmetic.

Before any model loading, a full production-controller rehearsal runs both simulated models through normal answering, all masks/cuts, sham/prefix/disconnection checks, development selection, frozen evaluation prediction, persistence and call accounting. Its outputs are explicitly synthetic and temporary. Simulated raw answering deliberately fails while graded routing succeeds, to check that the revised controller actually tests the revised question. Real-metadata tests check every tokenizer row, unsupported argmax rejection, graph disconnection and storage round trips. All future empirical controls still need to pass on actual models.

Relevant prior work, already reviewed in the preceding report: Zhao et al.2021 calibration (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/zhao21c.html), Geva et al.2023 attention-edge routing (https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.751/), Dai et al.2024 binding edits (https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.967/), Mixing Mechanisms (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06182), causal abstraction (https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04709), and recent routing/content comparisons (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11859). A positive route profile would need result-specific novelty review. We do not claim the first calibrated score, causal carrier test, relational representation or cross-model trajectory analysis.
