# Competing hypotheses and decisive tests

These mechanisms can coexist. A diagnostic must say which outcome would distinguish them; a single benchmark gain cannot do that. Sources: [L1–L8, L12–L13, L19–L21, L23](01_literature_map.md).

| Hypothesis | Differentiating prediction | Falsifier or serious counterevidence | Principal confound | Decisive feasible experiment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H1 Behavioral redistribution** | Post-training boosts `pass@1` and preferred traces; base reaches the same solution set under high-budget search/prefix prompting. Later adaptation differences disappear when exploration entropy and starting item profile are matched. | Verified new solution *classes* on held-out generated tasks remain inaccessible to base under matched compute/search, and are learned by post-trained model from subsequent examples. | Finite high-`k` cannot prove zero probability; temperature and length affect coverage. | Compare pass@1/16/64, unique verified algorithm classes and calibration at fixed token budget; train later on novel operator compositions. |
| **H2 Strategy acquisition** | A trained model succeeds on genuinely novel compositions and transfers the algorithm to changed surface form with few/no examples; trajectory traces show a verified new procedure. | Improvement collapses under surface change or is fully recovered from base with search/prefixes; later learning is no better. | Verifier only checks final answer; prior pretraining exposure opaque. | Procedural DSL with held-out composition rule, executable trace checker and counterfactual renaming; use a search-compute matched base. |
| **H3 Computational reorganization** | Same primitive competence but improved composition; causal activation/weight patching transfers composition while leaving primitives similar. | Composition gap vanishes after output-distribution controls; patches have no selective effect. | Representation similarity is not causal; patching may damage unrelated computations. | Small paired models, fixed probe set, path/activation patching on selected layers, negative-control patches, and recovery tests. Reserve for follow-up. |
| **H4 Plasticity transformation** | At comparable item-level initial profiles, one randomized history reliably changes the *absolute* downstream learning curve across more than one task family and update rule. | Well-powered contrasts are inside equivalence bounds, or all apparent effects track baseline/entropy/validation-loss proxies. | Baseline matching is post-treatment selection; optimizer interaction. | Factorial histories × two task families × full/LoRA; analyze total effect first, matched overlap second, with paired seeds. |
| **H5 Representation restructuring** | A prespecified representation statistic predicts held-out-family adaptation and a targeted intervention changes the response in the predicted direction. | Statistic fails held-out prediction or intervention changes it without changing learning. | Layer, token and probe choices can be cherry-picked; rank can follow scale changes. | Cheap CKA/effective-rank screen on fixed probes, then one causal weight/activation intervention if robust effect exists. |
| **H6 Distribution dependence** | Differences follow training–adaptation similarity and vanish/reverse with independent families or equalized exposure. | Same signed history effect recurs on prespecified distant families despite controls. | “Distant” is subjective; data generation can encode hidden similarities. | Cross history A/B with near/far procedural families, template/operator holdouts and explicit overlap measures. |
| **H7 Exploration bottleneck** | Later RL gain is predicted by pre-RL verified-solution diversity/high-`k` pass rate; full SFT and DPO that narrow support hinder RL even at equal pass@1. | Diversity-matched branches differ strongly in RL learning, or diversity does not mediate effect under an intervention. | Sampling temperature and answer length alter diversity. | Entropy/temperature controlled rollouts; compare history effect after randomized entropy-restoration intervention. |
| **H8 Optimizer-state/history artifact** | Apparent checkpoint effect changes markedly when Adam moments, schedulers or LoRA adapters are reset versus carried forward. | Effect remains after optimizer-state reset and equalized tuning sweep. | Reset may itself change early dynamics. | Fork each checkpoint into fresh-state and continued-state adaptation; same data order and LR grid. |

## Identification order

1. **First establish an outcome.** Randomized branch from identical base, sealed downstream generators, paired learning curves, item-level baseline profiles. Do not analyze mechanisms after a noisy single run.
2. **Test ordinary explanations.** Baseline competence, high-`k` solution coverage, entropy, validation loss, optimizer sensitivity, distribution distance and drift. Compare predictive performance on held-out history seeds/families.
3. **Only then intervene.** A correlation between gradient alignment and gains is a candidate, not evidence of mediation. For a mechanism, perturb the proposed variable, include sham perturbation and show predicted learning change without simply altering initial skill.

The favored H4 is rejected as a general statement if effects reverse with adaptation rule or if an equivalence analysis bounds all prespecified differences below the practical threshold. A task-dependent interaction would favor H6 and still be informative.
