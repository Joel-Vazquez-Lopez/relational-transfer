Opposing-team Patient questions failed direct reversed baselines on all 3/3 documents, so strict both-role success was 0/3 and the Patient patch outcomes are uninterpretable. A post-run audit also found that the reused system instruction requested a person's name while Patient requested a team. This supports a narrow cross-relation scorer-selection effect, not a two-role mechanism. Next choose explicit two-participant clauses, match answer types in the prompt, and require baseline competence before causal interpretation.

**2026-10-17 fresh-data update:** On [five new, document-disjoint MAVEN-Arg passages](FRESH_BINDING_REPORT.md), the locked Qwen 7B layer-16 question-label patch changed 9/10 complete role answers to the second event; both roles and all controls passed in 4/5 documents. A separate adaptive cross-document diagnostic yielded 8/10 second-event answers from a B/A donor and 10/10 first-event answers from an A/B donor, without copying donor names. The better working hypothesis is a transferable event-order cue followed by role-sensitive retrieval. This is narrower than an indivisible shared-event record and resembles existing binding/order mechanisms; test another relation and position-sensitive rivals before any novelty claim.

## Why this is the strongest bet

The new authentic-text panel establishes two useful facts: Gemma reaches 63/68 alias-rule answers, and an accessible open-weight Qwen 7B reaches 58/68. Thus the task is feasible both as a hosted behavioral study and as a local causal-development target. The small Qwen models often repeat one participant for Agent and Patient; Gemma and Qwen 7B usually keep them separate. Four of Gemma's five main-panel misses on Control relations disappear under adaptive commander-specific wording. See the [main replication](PANEL_V2_REPORT.md) and [open-model gate](OPEN_MODEL_GATE.md).

The present items are **too easy on event identity** for the most interesting hypothesis. Gemma, Qwen 7B, and Qwen 1.5B have zero scored wrong-event selections across 34 target events each; Qwen 0.5B has four event pairs with at least one wrong-event answer but no fully correct event pairs. This panel can compare role errors and wording effects, but cannot tell us whether a competent model selects one event before retrieving its roles, or independently finds each role from local cues.

## Proposed claim to test

> On natural passages with multiple same-type events, event identity and participant role behave as separable *causal controls* in a competent open-weight LLM; a restricted event-first account predicts held-out joint answers and intervention effects better than matched fieldwise, lexical, and position-based accounts.

“Separable controls” is a testable model of behavior and intervention effects. It is **not** an assertion that the model contains a literal two-slot record or a unique event-selector neuron. A failure would also be informative: matched cue mixtures may explain the outputs, or the model may stay at ceiling until the passages become too ambiguous to interpret.

## Smallest discriminating behavioral study

1. **Build new source-grounded cases.** Prefer independently adjudicated passages with three or more same-type events and distinct Agent/Patient entities, balancing defeat, command, and another relation. Keep natural source sentences; allow controlled sentence-order changes only when discourse remains grammatical and all labels stay valid. Split by document and event family. Freeze trigger boundaries, referent aliases, ambiguities, and question wording before model calls. The inspected six- and 17-document panels stay development-only.
2. **Cross the controls.** For every target event, ask Agent and Patient separately. Vary event cue (marker, quoted trigger, local context) and role wording (generic versus relation-specific) in a predeclared small factorial. Reverse event order in a matched presentation condition. All conditions must request the same gold participant where semantics are unchanged. Score four-way entity identity, joint event tuples, wrong-event borrowing, wrong-role answers, format violations, and abstentions at the document level.
3. **Fit restricted rivals on separate development documents.** A position-only rule, a local lexical/nearest-trigger rule, a fieldwise role selector, and a shared event selector should each predict the *joint* response categories on held-out documents. Give the cue-mixture rivals comparable flexibility. The core discriminator is whether the two roles move together to one alternative event under event-cue pressure more often than the fitted fieldwise model predicts, after conditioning on salience and position.
4. **Test Gemma and open-weight Qwen 7B.** Gemma checks whether the behavioral effect survives a strong hosted model. Qwen 7B supplies the accessible model for internal intervention. Require enough non-ceiling but still interpretable Qwen 7B cases before patching; a panel on which it merely guesses is also unusable.

## Causal study only after behavioral discrimination

Use a 2×2 query grid for each well-solved passage: event A/B × Agent/Patient. At matched sites in Qwen 7B, compare an event-cue donor, a role-cue donor, and a donor changing both. A useful crossover would transfer event identity across both role queries while preserving role, and transfer role across both event queries while preserving event. Measure *new gold answers*, wrong→correct and correct→wrong transitions, and full answer-distribution shifts. Use same-token and position controls, lexical/candidate-frequency controls, and matched prompt lengths where feasible. Patch locations and layers must be selected on development items and evaluated on unseen documents.

A patch that merely swaps the final answer is insufficient. It may carry answer identity or instruction state instead of a reusable event variable. A causal claim requires a prediction the matched copier and cue-mixture accounts fail, preferably after the diagnostic is calibrated on learned controls with known opposite routes.

## Decision gates

- **Pursue an event-first account** only if it outpredicts matched rivals on held-out joint error categories and makes successful cross-role intervention predictions in Qwen 7B. Replicate a behavioral prediction in Gemma and on a second source.
- **Pursue a narrower interface account** if relation-specific wording consistently repairs errors on fresh command events while event identity stays stable. Existing prompt and evaluation literature means this needs a new predictive law or intervention, not merely another score gain.
- **Stop the mechanism claim** if no controlled condition produces identifiable wrong-event events in a competent model, if lexical/position mixtures match the joint predictions, or if patches only move the answer token without the predicted cross-role structure. Report the bounded benchmark result and negative mechanistic test.

## Literature boundary

Multi-event argument extraction and event confusion are established topics: [TabEAE](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.701/) extracts co-occurring events, and [EAESR](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.328/) explicitly addresses cross-event confusion. [REGen](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.649/) already shows why exact span matching underestimates generative argument extraction. These block claims that paired-event extraction, prompt wording, or relaxed scoring alone are new. The possible gap is the **held-out, rival-predictive causal test** of how event and role controls combine in a competent model. That gap needs a focused full-text novelty audit before paper positioning. The prior [Astra reboot](../astra_reboot_2026-10-16/REPORT.md) independently proposed the shared-event versus fieldwise distinction; this memo makes the current natural-panel gate and open-model result explicit.
