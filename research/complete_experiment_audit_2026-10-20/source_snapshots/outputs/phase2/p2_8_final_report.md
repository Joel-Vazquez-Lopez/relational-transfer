# P2.8 — Residual Invariance and Reuse Stress Test

## Executive summary and validity

**VALID; P2.8-A.** All 23 final validity gates passed. The frozen P2.7 R3 and its M1 residual construction replayed exactly (73,728 residual rows; 84 R3 model×split rows; maximum absolute difference zero). No protected seed was accessed, no task network was trained, and no new network forward pass occurred.

## Scientific question, evidence, and claim boundary

P2.7 showed that an eight-coefficient undifferentiated correction R3 predicted M1 residuals better than the tested endpoint/event factorization. P2.8 asks whether that exact R3 rule reuses when task-native dimensions are withheld, not whether it is a mechanism, primitive, predictive state, operator, or grammar. D5 remains the acquisition-side causal anchor; no causal mechanism is inferred here.

## Exact R3 replay, definition, and residual provenance

R3 has eight fitted coefficients: separate endpoint/event demand indicators plus six fixed semantic projections. It retains the frozen P2.7 feature encoding, source-only RMS preprocessing, ridge alpha grid and selection, and M1 score-residual target. Every source core residual was generated by grouped OOF M1; every noncore residual came from an M1 fit that never saw that row. Replay details and hashes are in `p2_7_r3_replay.json`.

## Invariance definition, preregistered axes, source/target partitions, and algebra

Reuse means predicting the correct residual consequence in a target domain without target fitting, scaling, centering, or calibration—not numerical equality before and after transformation. The prefit `invariance_prereg.json` freezes all source/target masks. Identity holds out endpoints 4–5 within the observed/observed support class; event directions are separate; R-family q0→q1 is a semantic role reversal with matched query support status. Whole contexts and candidate configurations are complete structural holdouts. The crossed test withholds identity group, query event, and candidate configuration simultaneously. The 4-way identity×event recombination is separate. Every within-model source and target score row is disjoint; predictors contain no archived target score. All 108 axis×model algebra checks are SAFE. The whole-context crossed variant was rejected prefit because condition A had only four target score rows.

## Baselines, complexity, and normalization

B0 is M1 alone (zero residual prediction); B1 is frozen P2.7 R1; B3 is frozen P2.7 R3. R1 and R3 each fit eight coefficients with source-only regularization and preprocessing. Normalized residual RMSE is target RMSE divided by B0 RMSE on exactly the same target rows; 1 means no gain, below 1 benefit, above 1 harm. Raw RMSE/MAE and source-OOF descriptive retention are in the model table. No new predictor or capacity search was introduced.

## Crossed holdout and compositional recombination

| Test | Target score cells | R1 mean NRMSE | R3 mean NRMSE | R3<1 models | R3<R1 models |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| recombination_D2 | 6534 | 0.6284 | 0.5111 | 12/12 | 12/12 |
| crossed_D3 | 1098 | 13.5871 | 14.7947 | 0/12 | 0/12 |

The crossed target combines unseen identity group, query event, and candidate configuration while holding query/candidate support at observed/observed. The recombination target consists of the withheld off-diagonal identity×event conjunctions. Neither was selected using residual performance. Crucially, R3 is severely harmful on the strongest cross: mean normalized RMSE 14.7947 (M1 alone = 1), with 0/12 model benefits. Mean raw residual RMSE increases from about 3.98 for M1 alone to 58.73 with R3. This is not a small loss of retention; it is a pronounced extrapolation failure.

## Frozen negative controls

The broken-correspondence control applies a fixed cyclic permutation to endpoint identities in target R3 features only; the residual target is unchanged. The source-residual control permutes source residual labels within strict model/family/kind/support/demand/split strata, then refits the same eight-coefficient R3.

| Test | R3 mean NRMSE | Broken identity correspondence | Source residual permutation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| recombination_D2 | 0.5111 | 0.4949 | 0.5296 |
| crossed_D3 | 14.7947 | 14.8175 | 15.0897 |

The successful recombination test does NOT pass both specificity controls: broken identity correspondence is slightly better than R3, and residual permutation is only 0.0185 NRMSE worse, below the frozen 0.02 materiality threshold. Both controls are worse on the crossed test, but R3 itself fails badly there. The frozen Boolean decision rule checks a beneficial recombination or crossed result and materially worse controls on a recombination or crossed result separately; it does not require those conditions to hold on the same test. The mechanical P2.8-A label must therefore not be read as establishing successful semantic-correspondence-specific reuse on the strong cross. All single-axis control results are in `negative_controls.csv`.

## Frozen classification, licensed conclusion, and surviving explanations

The mechanical frozen category is **P2.8-A**. The licensed positive result is bounded: the unchanged R3 improves on all six single-axis tests and on the two-factor identity×event recombination. It does not generalize to the strongest three-way cross, and the successful recombination lacks control specificity. Because the frozen Boolean rule allows different tests to satisfy the success and control clauses, the category is broader than the strongest evidence. No cross-model category is assigned. This experiment does not identify a neural or causal mechanism, transformer primitive, grammar, operator, predictive state, or universal law. The surviving explanation set includes bounded ordinary-axis reuse and domain-dependent compact interpolation; support-topology transfer is described but not given a causal interpretation. The most informative unresolved disagreement is the gap between ordinary single-axis/recombination success and catastrophic identity×event×configuration failure; it cannot be repaired by adding features or changing holdouts after seeing it.
