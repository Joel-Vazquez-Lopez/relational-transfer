Simply changing the pool to triples with two H identities would be unsafe: the third candidate in the existing S rule can become an H–H meaning, creating negative-candidate exposure to a nominally withheld relation. ## Stage 1 — candidate intervention definitions, not frozen generators

All candidate conditions would draw supervised queries only from the same 48 H–G/G–H meanings, with exact event, source/target-role, identity, correct-position, example-count, renderer, and update matching. No condition may put any H–H meaning in a query or any candidate slot. Candidate-only shortcut validity would be checked over complete choice tensors, with each candidate meaning rotated through the correct position so that candidate information alone cannot reveal the answer.

The intervention templates are:

- **R (focal relational comparison):** for a query containing one H and one G endpoint, include a foil that substitutes a second H identity in the same endpoint role while holding the G anchor, event, and remaining structured factors fixed. For example, `(h_i,g_j,e)` competes with `(h_k,g_j,e)`, or `(g_j,h_i,e)` with `(g_j,h_k,e)`. The choice set must not contain `(h_i,h_k,e)` or any other H–H meaning.
- **E (marginal exposure control):** keep the same H/G meanings and marginal role frequencies, but do not systematically place the focal H identity alternatives in the same anchored comparison. Remaining foils are drawn from a balanced, preregistered non-focal schedule.
- **D (generic-difficulty control):** use non-focal hard alternatives, such as G-identity substitutions or renderer-valid near-competitive foils, while preserving the same supervised support and marginal exposure. It must not systematically require the R focal H-identity distinction.

These definitions specify the intended contrast but are not a complete executable schedule: the E/D foil allocation, exact hyperedge multiset, and per-seed balancing algorithm have not been solved or verified. They are intentionally not represented as frozen generators.

## Stage 2 — hostile identification audit

### Leakage and support

Literal query-set omission is insufficient. Every generated example must be audited across its message/query, selected candidate, and all foils. H–H candidate scenes are disallowed because they can provide negative supervision even when never selected as the query. The existing qset rule must not be reused with two-H triples without changing/revalidating its candidate construction. R’s intended H-vs-H competition can be presented on a shared G anchor without an H–H candidate, but that shared-anchor training still supplies structured indirect evidence about the H identities. It is a permitted treatment only if the estimand is stated as this precise comparison-graph difference, not “no information about H–H” in an absolute sense.

### Marginal matching and candidate-set structure

Exact matching of positive queries and per-meaning candidate counts is combinatorially possible in principle while changing candidate co-occurrence. But the required three-way schedule has not been constructed. The complete query-to-foil and candidate-set/hyperedge tables must be audited, including answer position, correct/foil status, ordered-pair, event, source/target role, identity exposure, geometry keys, and train/evaluation overlap. Matching only totals would leave the main intervention confounded with other changes to the choice-set multiset.

### Generic difficulty — critical unresolved confound

R necessarily changes which alternatives compete. D can put hard alternatives elsewhere (for example, among G identities), so it can manipulate generic difficulty without deliberately repeating R’s focal H-identity contrast. But “generic discriminative pressure” is not a single model-independent quantity. Initial cross-entropy, entropy, correct–foil margin, gradient norm, and effective competitor count are non-equivalent; gradient direction and its alignment to identity-specific parameters matter even when scalar norms match. All of these can change during learning.

An initialization-only match would not establish matched optimization pressure over training. A match on early-training loss/gradients is an outcome of the intervention and cannot be guaranteed by a static generator; conditioning or selecting D examples/checkpoints to force such a match would make the treatment adaptive and could erase or distort the effect under study. No prespecified small set of pressure measures and defensible equivalence tolerances is presently supported by P3.2/A1. Selecting the metric after seeing R/D results would be unacceptable. The design therefore cannot yet license “beyond generic difficulty.”

The required question—whether R and D can be adequately pressure-matched—cannot be answered from the current archived inputs and structural checks alone. Answering it would require empirical calibration of the proposed D pressure against paired learning trajectories. P4 explicitly forbids pilot training in this authorization. It would be improper to pretend an initialization scalar or a post hoc metric resolves this.

### Other readiness items

- Sample-size justification must use the seed/model as the inferential unit. Discovery effects cannot serve as confirmatory effect estimates. No smallest meaningful P4 effect or defensible seed-level variance bound has been frozen; choosing both after outcomes would compromise the design.
- Paired initialization and dense checkpoints are compatible with the model framework and would support later developmental description, but they do not solve the R-vs-D pressure confound. Dense traces should be retained for every update/checkpoint according to a fixed schedule, not selectively around a divergence.
- P3.2/A1 do not provide P4-specific developmental trajectories. Their artifacts can inform implementation/provenance, not identify an appropriate difficulty-equivalence margin for this new intervention.
