# Targeted literature check and novelty boundary

19 October 2026. This was a targeted check of primary sources, not a systematic review or proof that no prior paper studies construction-dependent competence filtering.

- [Zhao et al.,2021, Calibrate Before Use](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/zhao21c.html) already demonstrates answer biases, prompt-dependent accuracy and improvements from external calibration. Our scalar threshold is not a new calibration method, and should not be equated with their specific content-free-input estimator.
- [Zhang & Nanda,2024, Towards Best Practices of Activation Patching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16042) already shows that evaluation metrics and corruption methods can change interpretability results. We cannot claim discovery of the general fact that measurement choices matter.
- [Wei et al.,2026, From Parameters to Answers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11859) is relevant prior work on knowledge retrieval/use. Availability versus native answer production is not a novel conceptual distinction.
- [Dai et al.,2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.967/) and [Mixing Mechanisms](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06182v2) already study causal binding/retrieval and competing mechanisms. Our period result is a constraint on our own carrier interpretation.

The candidate empirical question is narrower: does a response-dependent competence screen systematically alter the linguistic construction mixture and then change the causal conclusions admitted by a cross-model study? The present data establish the sample-admission difference for two checkpoints on a small synthetic panel. They do not establish a resulting change in inferred causal mechanism, broader prevalence, or priority over existing work. A prospective study and a dedicated literature review are needed before a novelty or paper claim.

No p values were computed after searching for a favorable endpoint. No additional inference follows this literature check.
