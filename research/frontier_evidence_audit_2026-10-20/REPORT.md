# What survives the research programme

20 October 2026. **Decision: NARROW for a replicated measurement result; NO-GO for the currently proposed linguistic mechanisms.** This is a critical synthesis and independent replay of saved scalar records, not a new model experiment. No hypotheses, thresholds, intervention sites or original decisions were changed.

## The result to build around

**On our fixed linguistic panel, the answer rule changes which constructions pass a correctness screen; screening then changes the reported cross-checkpoint intervention comparison, although the underlying model scores and interventions are unchanged.**

This is the strongest completed prospective finding. It is narrower than identifying how meaning develops. It is useful because our original Observatory was in danger of interpreting a selected sample as a property of the models' computational development.

The earlier answer-rule/selection synthesis generated this hypothesis. The subsequent confirmation froze its predictions before collecting 24 fresh lexical families, eight prompts per family, on each of two local checkpoints. Those families share construction templates and were authored and annotated by AI; they are not a random natural-language sample.

| Checkpoint/construction | Native correct answers /96 | Fixed-threshold correct /96 | Native complete four-answer units /24 | Fixed-threshold complete /24 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Qwen 7B active | 55 | 89 | 3 | 19 |
| Qwen 7B passive | 95 | 87 | 23 | 18 |
| Llama 3B active | 69 | 91 | 7 | 20 |
| Llama 3B passive | 78 | 86 | 13 | 17 |

The threshold came from earlier exposed calibration examples and was held fixed; it was not fitted to these 24 families. It defines an **external classifier**, not improved native model behavior or a decoded internal posterior. In particular, Qwen passive performance becomes worse under this rule. We must report those losses with the gains.

The sole existing confirmatory test compared the change in active admission with the change in passive admission, at the lexical-family level, for Qwen. Nineteen differences were positive, zero negative and five tied; its registered one-sided exact sign-test p value is 0.00000190735. This replay reproduces that calculation; it adds no new significance test. The interpretation depends on independent/exchangeable family signs under the null. Shared templates and authored lexical choices limit population inference. Repeated layers, answers and masks are not independent replicates.

## Why this affects a computational comparison

The original confirmation used one fixed cut, block 14, and a source-isolated period route. Its endpoint is the role×question contrast after the mask divided by the native contrast, with the frozen denominator guard |D_native| ≥ 0.5. It is a **retained score contrast under a specified graph intervention**, not a fraction of semantic information or a direct measure of processing time. Ratios can exceed one.

| Sample admission policy | Mean-retention gap, Llama minus Qwen |
|---|---:|
| All units with valid per-model denominators | 0.0718 |
| Each model's native-complete units | 0.5860 |
| Each model's fixed-threshold-complete units | 0.0401 |

These are different conditional estimands. The large selected gap is real for that selected population; it does not establish a model-family law. No model changed between the rows. A common native gate also leaves a gap of 0.5848. On strictly shared valid-denominator support, selection changes the gap from 0.0639 to 0.5848. Thus unequal denominator support alone does not explain the shift.

The previous supplementary decomposition assigns 0.4237 of the own-selected shift of 0.5143 to changed active/passive weights and 0.0905 to selection within constructions, using full-panel construction means as the reference. That reference-dependent arithmetic describes the composition of a reported mean; it is not causal mediation inside a Transformer. Existing leave-one-family-out shifts remain positive, 0.4262–0.5482. These analyses were descriptive follow-through, not additional confirmatory tests.

**Practical consequence:** compare the same declared linguistic population, show construction strata, and retain native behavior separately from external decoding. A success-conditioned question can be legitimate, but its answer must remain conditional. Do not silently turn it into a statement about a whole model.

This collection does **not** imply that models lack relational representations. It shows that our candidate descriptors and interventions have not identified a unique reusable linguistic variable. An untested route is not a negative result, and a technical failure is not evidence of absent competence.

## The identification problem that more plots cannot solve

In the binary source task, let R be which of two people is the actor, Q which directional proposition is queried, and T whether the proposition is entailed. With the experiment's coding, T = 1[R = Q]. Consequently, R can be reconstructed from (Q,T), and T from (R,Q).

An answer-conditioned representation and a relation representation can therefore encode interchangeable information on this support. Recovering R with a probe, finding a role×question score interaction, removing one output axis, or matching response directions does not by itself choose between those accounts. The replay includes all four cells of this logical certificate. This is a property of the design, not evidence that Qwen implements a particular program.

There is a second boundary: an unrestricted syntax-aware copier that parses an event record and retrieves its requested field can be functionally equivalent to what we call event selection. No answer-only test can distinguish descriptions with identical predictions. We must restrict the competing implementations and specify a native internal dependency where their predictions differ. Merely adding events or roles does not prove identifiability.

The most useful reformulation of the original vision is therefore: **compare changes in causal dependencies across depth, with a separately validated measurement of those dependencies.** A shared-looking line becomes interpretable only when it predicts an intervention beyond output, surface-order and generic-depth baselines. The current record does not supply that validation.

## Prior work: what we cannot call new

This targeted primary-source check was refreshed on 20 October; it is not an exhaustive novelty review.

- [Zhao et al. (2021)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/zhao21c.html) already establish prompt-dependent answer biases and external calibration. Our fixed scalar boundary is not a new calibration method.
- [Jenner et al. (2024)](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/37d9f19150fce07bced2a81fc87d47a6-Paper-Conference.pdf), §2.2 and Appendix H, explicitly discuss filtering that changes chess task structure and the strength of interpretability results. Selection changing an interpreted population is not our invention.
- [Zhang & Nanda (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16042) show that metrics and corruption methods can change activation-patching conclusions. Here the specific controlled difference is admission policy on unchanged scores and interventions.
- [Davies, Jiang & Zhai, CALM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00333) already distinguish linguistic representations from their causal use and evaluate alignment with a task causal model. We should not present that conceptual separation as a new Gallistel-derived framework.
- [Dai, Heinzerling & Inui (2024)](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.967/) localize low-rank binding-related information and edit retrieval. Our steering and threshold results do not identify a rival binding mechanism or establish priority over that work.

The possible contribution is the **specific prospective demonstration connecting construction-dependent readout, complete-unit admission, and a changed cross-checkpoint intervention comparison**, with raw outputs and failed controls. Whether it is sufficiently new and general for a paper is still open. A small p value answers neither question.

## My research decision

**Preserve and write up the completed measurement finding as the central result of this stage.** A defensible working title is *Readout Rules and Sample Admission in Causal Comparisons of Language Models*. The source-mask and donor tests belong in the evidence showing why we narrowed the claim, rather than being repackaged as a successful binding mechanism.

For the original Observatory, retain the aim of tracking availability, use and behavioral expression through layers and components. Gallistel's functional distinction is useful inspiration, but neither his temporal equations nor a neat serial layer assignment is established by our results. The future mechanistic target must be a functional dependency with a separating prediction; [NEXT_DESIGN.md](NEXT_DESIGN.md) specifies the two useful routes and their prerequisites.

I would not collect another prompt-format pilot or search for a better-looking layer now. The most valuable remaining paper check is external validity: does a comparable admission effect materially change an existing, independently designed causal analysis? Until that is tested or established from released artifacts, describe this as a reproducible methodological case study, not a general law or a demonstrated novel circuit.

## Audit scope and reproduction

`replay.py` uses only the Python standard library. Run `python3 research/frontier_evidence_audit_2026-10-20/replay.py` from the project root. It rereads all 1,562 source scalar records, recomputes the 96 four-answer units, both answer rules, the original sign test and six fixed-mask admission comparisons, and checks agreement with the original results. It verifies the legend diagnostic's summary counts and the binary design aliasing. It does not reopen full tensors; the original independently replayed full-vector audits remain the evidence for numerical collection validity.

`REPLAY.json` records every check and `RECOMPUTED_UNITS.csv` every recomputed unit. `SOURCE_MANIFEST.json` records hashes of recent reports and explicitly used records. The older 57-report archive was assessed through its existing handoff inventory and foundation index; all 57 historical raw studies were not independently reproduced here. The recent inventory includes multiple versions of one interrupted factorial, not multiple independent experiments. Sources remain unchanged. No new model inference, calibration fit, adaptive experiment, model download, training or paid API call occurred in this audit.
