# Brain-O — Synthetic Binding and Query Routing (15 October 2026)

**Status: exploratory, scratch-trained synthetic Transformers only. Not a pretrained-LLM mechanism or novelty claim.**

## Evidence

Three independently initialized 2-layer causal Transformers (48 hidden dimensions, 4 heads, 500 training steps) processed two structured synthetic events followed by an AGENT/PATIENT and verb query. Each model was evaluated on 600 held-out generated cases per overlap condition.

| Overlap type | Seed 1015 | Seed 1016 | Seed 1017 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Disjoint entities | 53.67% | 51.33% | 52.33% |
| Shared agent | 73.50% | 76.50% | 75.33% |
| Shared patient | 76.17% | 73.33% | 74.83% |
| Cross-role A→P | 50.83% | 50.17% | 52.83% |
| Cross-role P→A | 47.50% | 48.67% | 51.33% |

This is consistent with a simple **role-marginal shortcut**: infer agent or patient but neglect the association between that role and the *queried verb*. In seed 1016, changing only the role query changed 100% of disjoint-case model answers, but changing only the queried verb changed 0.67% when the gold answer must change. This is behavioral evidence, not proof of specific internal network states.

**Training controls:** A 1600-step continuation (seed 1015) retained disjoint accuracy 54.83% and cross-role accuracies 49.00%/48.50%. A 4-layer model trained 700 steps (seed 1015) remained at 49.50% disjoint and 49.17%/48.33% cross-role. These runs are not compute-matched. Showing both verb queries during training and adding a query-contrastive loss also did **not** improve ambiguous condition accuracy in matched two-seed trials. Mean over disjoint + two cross-role conditions:
- Seed 1015: paired CE 49.22%, paired + contrast 50.17%.
- Seed 1016: paired CE 49.17%, paired + contrast 50.00%.

## Binding-square diagnostic

For two verb-indexed events G={(A,V1,B),(C,V2,D)}, define S to swap the two patients without changing entity, role or verb marginals. A correct event-specific patient-answer system must satisfy:
- f(G,V1) = f(SG,V2)
- f(G,V2) = f(SG,V1)
- f(G,V1) != f(G,V2)

The last inequality rules out trivial constant-output passes. On 600 disjoint pairs for seed 1016, the trained model achieved 0 nondegenerate binding squares, although the two equalities alone were satisfied in 58.83% of cases. **A local surface parser could pass the square too; it is not a causal reuse diagnostic.**

## Mathematical proposal — binding fibres

Project an event graph G to its role marginals M(G), forgetting which agent-patient pair belongs to which indexed event. For n uniquely assigned patients and n agent/verb slots, a fixed M can correspond to n! distinct pairings. Under the uniform pairing prior, H(Pairing | M) = log2(n!) bits. A model accessing only M cannot identify a requested pairing better than 1/n. This is an application of standard information theory, **not a novel theorem**, and does not bound real Transformers that can still consult source tokens.

## Decisive follow-ups

1. Create a **trainable positive-control** architecture that passes the same task (>95% and nondegenerate binding square), holding vocabulary and training distribution fixed.
2. Audit hidden-state sensitivity to the verb query, separate routing failure from unavailable event-specific relational information with causal path interventions.
3. Use novel human-validated 3+ participant natural-language examples with position/role/verb independently crossed; include positional and lexical shortcuts.
4. Novelty check: binding, mechanism mixing, shortcut selection, compositional attention circuits and equivariance are active prior art. Do not claim novelty based solely on our diagnostic.
