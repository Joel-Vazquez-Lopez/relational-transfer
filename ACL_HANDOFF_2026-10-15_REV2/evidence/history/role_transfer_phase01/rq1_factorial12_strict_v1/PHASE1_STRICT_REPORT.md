# Strict Phase 1 scientific report — 14 October 2026

## Question and prospective design

The initial 12-entity factorial suggested an approximately ten-point
common-partner penalty but missed its frozen success criterion. It also left
simple four-edge paths unmatched. This independent, prospectively frozen
follow-up uses eight new nonisomorphic anchor graph classes and three new
paired seeds per cell (96 models). Within each family, the four graph cells
cross one versus two common partners with three versus four simple
three-edge paths. Degree, training-pair count, total triangles, both focal
triangle counts, simple four-edge paths, and four-cycle count are matched.
Every cell is at most two degree-preserving switches from its anchor.

All 96 planned models completed without technical failure. Checkpoints are
unique; saved model hashes, manifest hashes, protocol hashes, and run ledger
were verified. The training pool and 320-rendering focal evaluation passed
the frozen validity audit.

## Prespecified outcome

| Contrast | Mean focal binary-role difference | 95% family t interval | Exact two-sided p | Holm-adjusted p |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Two versus one common partners | −9.94 points | −20.46 to +0.58 | 0.0781 | 0.1563 |
| Four versus three three-edge paths | +3.82 points | −4.88 to +12.52 | 0.3750 | 0.3750 |

The exploratory interaction was +7.59 points (95% interval −8.89 to +24.07,
exact p=0.2969). Minimum familiar four-choice accuracy across models was
99.58%, so ordinary task-learning failure does not explain the focal
difference. The second prespecified rendering suite gave a similar
common-partner difference of −9.95 points (unadjusted exact p=0.0703).

**The frozen practical-success criterion was not met.** The directional
common-partner estimate resembles the initial factorial almost exactly, but
its family-level uncertainty is too wide for a confirmatory claim. The
three-edge-path effect and interaction are not established.

## Cross-study interpretation

The initial and strict cohorts each estimated a −9.94-point common-partner
effect on eight different graph families. Thirteen of the 16 family effects
were negative. An *exploratory*, post hoc pooled analysis gives −9.94 points,
95% family t interval −16.34 to −3.54, and exact sign-flip p=0.00424.
This pooled test was not specified before the first study and combines
designs with different matching rules. It is useful evidence of a recurring
pattern, not a replacement for either cohort's failed prespecified test.

The wide strict-cohort family effects (−30.0 to +5.3 points) are central.
Some graph families show a large role-transfer failure while others are near
ceiling. This means average accuracy alone is inadequate and the proposed
structure × objective interaction cannot yet be interpreted without
addressing family heterogeneity and ceiling effects. Even the stricter graph
match cannot rule out every changed local motif or identify an internal
neural mechanism.

## Decision

Do not claim a confirmed shared-partner mechanism or start the Phase 2
objective comparison under the current confirmation gate. Preserve both
factorials, including all family effects and negative tests. A defensible
next controlled step is a separately frozen, larger independent graph
cohort with a prospective family count and explicit sensitivity to ceiling
effects. If that supports a stable structural contrast, test the planned
training-objective × structure interaction. The pretrained-LLM bridge must
still be tested separately; these synthetic results do not establish it.
